Former teacher and assistant softball coach at Pamlico High School in Pamlico County, North Carolina. Held a temporary full-time teaching contract that ran through June 12, 2019. Age 33 at the time of the documented offense conduct. Victim age and sex are not stated in available sources; victim age is recorded as 18 here as an inferred high-school upper-bound estimate (a current student during the offense period, described as a former student by the time of arrest).

The documented offense period ran from March 24 through June 11, 2019, the final months of Walton's teaching contract. The school district notified Pamlico County Sheriff Chris Davis on June 14, 2019, two days after Walton's contract ended. Walton turned herself in to law enforcement on August 22, 2019.

She was charged in August 2019 with one felony count of sex act with a student and one felony count of indecent liberties with a student, both under North Carolina's educator-specific framework. Pamlico County District Attorney Scott Thomas's office prosecuted; the case was assigned in Pamlico County Superior Court.

On June 9, 2020, Walton entered a guilty plea before Judge Joshua W. Willey Jr. to one count of crime against nature — North Carolina's general criminal-conduct statute, distinct from the educator-specific framework under which the case was originally charged. The District Attorney dismissed both original educator-specific felonies without leave, with no plea agreement linking the dismissal to the crime-against-nature plea. Judge Willey imposed a community disposition under North Carolina structured sentencing: a five-to-fifteen-month confinement range suspended in favor of thirty-six months of supervised probation, with the supervision transferable to Craven County, plus a DNA sample.

NOTE: This case fits the same charge-restructuring pattern observed in Umphlett #679 — North Carolina educator-specific statutes dismissed by the District Attorney in favor of a guilty plea to the broader crime-against-nature statute. In both cases the educator-specific framework, which is normally the heart of an NC teacher-student case, was abandoned in favor of a more generally-applicable conviction; in both cases the disposition produced no active incarceration. The clustering of these two cases in adjacent eastern North Carolina counties (Pamlico and Currituck) and the similar plea-structure outcome may reflect a regional prosecutorial pattern worth flagging for cross-case comparison.
