Alexandria Kaluzny #864 sentenced

StatePennsylvaniaCourtLycoming County Court of Common Pleas

Former student teacher at Jersey Shore Area Middle School (Jersey Shore Area School District), Jersey Shore, Pennsylvania. Final-semester student from Mansfield University completing her education degree during spring 2017. Age 24 at start of offense.

Victim was a 14-year-old male student in her class. No sexual contact occurred during the student teaching placement. After completing her assignment in spring 2017, she and the student began exchanging social media messages; the relationship became sexual over the summer of 2017 and continued through August 2018. Sexual encounters occurred multiple times in her vehicle near the Pine Creek Rail Trail and behind a garage in the Jersey Shore area. Explicit photographs and messages were found on the student's phone. During the 2017–18 school year, Kaluzny worked as a substitute teacher in the Bellefonte area.

A family member reported suspicions to authorities in summer 2018; the student had denied an inappropriate relationship when confronted by school officials the prior year. Turned herself in September 20, 2018. Originally charged with seven felonies and one misdemeanor including involuntary deviate sexual intercourse, statutory sexual assault, and unlawful contact with a minor. Pled guilty May 2021 to three counts of indecent assault and one count of statutory sexual assault. Sentenced September 30, 2021 by Judge Marc F. Lovecchio: 2 to 10 years in state prison; Megan's Law registration for 25 years. Judge recommended parole at first opportunity. Defense attorney Michael J. Rudinski; investigation led by Lycoming County Chief Detective William Weber.

NOTE: Victim's family filed a federal civil suit (U.S. Middle District of Pennsylvania) against Kaluzny, Jersey Shore Area School District, Assistant Principal Justin Armbruster, and Principal Laura Malark, alleging breach of duty to notify parents when school staff became aware of Kaluzny's activities. Family relocated to Maryland due to unwanted attention; action was stayed pending criminal proceedings. Conduct occurred entirely post-placement — she was never a district employee, only a placed student teacher.
