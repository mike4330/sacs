Former teacher's aide in the Mooresville School System in Mooresville, Indiana (Morgan County). Dismissed by the district before criminal charges were filed. Age approximately 36 at the time of the documented offense conduct in spring 2015; 38 at sentencing in August 2016. The case involves multiple male student victims across multiple incidents and venues; police publicly indicated they believed additional victims were likely. Forensic inspection of Moore's phone recovered nude photographs and explicit sexual content corroborating the conduct.

The youngest documented victim was 13 years old when the conduct began (14 by Moore's August 2015 arrest). They first connected on Instagram and Snapchat, and Moore sent him nude photographs three or four times across both platforms beginning in April 2015. Three documented physical-contact incidents followed. In the first, Moore picked the victim up, drove around looking for what she described as a good spot, returned to his driveway, and touched him sexually. In the second, she invited him to her bedroom, undressed both of them, and performed sexual acts on him. In the third, she picked him up from baseball practice, drove him to the rear of Newby Elementary School, and made sexual contact with him in the school parking area.

A second documented victim was a 14-year-old friend of the first victim. After he flirted with Moore on Instagram, she sent him upwards of fifteen nude photographs via Snapchat.

In June 2015 Moore arranged through Instagram for two 17-year-old victims — one she had been talking to online, and a friend he brought along — to come to her house, where she performed sexual acts on both.

A separate allegation involved a 16-year-old student at Tri-West High School. According to a friend of Moore's who witnessed her showing shirtless photographs to high-school boys, Moore had been sexually involved with that student as well; the allegation surfaced during the investigation but was not part of the formal charging set.

Moore was arrested on August 5, 2015. Morgan County Prosecuting Attorney Steve Sonnega's office originally filed three counts of sexual misconduct with a minor and three counts of dissemination of matter harmful to minors. On August 24, 2016, Moore pleaded guilty to one count of sexual misconduct with a minor (the Indiana Level 5 felony covering sexual touching) and three counts of dissemination of matter harmful to minors (Indiana Level 6 felonies). Two additional sexual-misconduct counts — one at the more serious Level 4 grade for intercourse or other sexual conduct, and one at the Level 5 grade — were dismissed pursuant to the plea. The defense presented mental-health mitigation at sentencing; the victims' mothers spoke against that argument, calling it an excuse.

Indiana Superior Court Judge Pete Foley sentenced Moore the same day. On the sexual-misconduct count he imposed a three-year sentence with one year suspended, leaving two years to serve and a statutory floor of at least 18 months before good-time credit could apply, followed by one year of home detention. The dissemination counts ran one year each, concurrent. Sex-offender registration was required upon release.

The post-sentencing record reflects two procedural events worth noting. In June 2017, approximately ten months into the two-year prison term, the state Department of Correction filed a notice that Moore was ineligible for the Community Transition Program — an early-release pathway that places offenders on home detention before the formal end of sentence. Judge Foley overrode the Department's ineligibility determination on July 11, 2017 and granted Community Transition Program placement. Within two months Moore violated the sex-offender residence conditions of that placement: her chosen address was too close to one of the victims' addresses. The probation department petitioned on September 1, 2017 to return her from home detention to the Morgan County Jail. At a September 7, 2017 hearing Judge Foley found the violation but gave Moore twenty days to find a compliant residence; she produced one within a day, and was released from jail back to community-transition placement on September 8. Defense attorney Kevin Karimi represented her at the violation hearing.

NOTE: Multiple-victim case spanning several venues, including the unusual on-campus locus at the rear of Newby Elementary School where one of the youngest-victim incidents occurred. The most-serious original count (Level 4 — intercourse or other sexual conduct) was dismissed at plea; the conviction count is at Level 5 (sexual touching), and contact_type 1 follows from that conviction-charge framing per project convention even though the underlying conduct documented in the probable-cause record went well beyond touching. Judge Foley's override of the state Department of Correction's Community Transition Program ineligibility determination is the case's standout procedural detail — the prison system itself recommended against early release, and the trial court released her anyway. Effective time in custody was approximately eleven months of a nominal two-year prison term.
