Former language-arts teacher at Williams Intermediate School in Pell City, Alabama (St. Clair County). Married, mother of two young children. Six years at Williams Intermediate; eight years total in teaching. Age 31 at the time of the offense conduct in late 2014; 32 at her April 2015 arrest. Two known male student victims, ages 15 and 18 at the time of the offenses. Notably, neither victim attended Williams Intermediate — both were students at Pell City High School. The institutional connection runs through Gilley's membership in the Pell City school system as a whole rather than through direct teacher-student classroom contact.

The documented offense conduct occurred away from school grounds, outside school hours, and over a span of time beginning in approximately November 2014. Pell City High School Resource Officer Jessie Burgos filed the initial complaint after coordinating with St. Clair County District Attorney Richard Minor and Pell City Superintendent Dr. Michael Barber. Gilley turned herself in to Pell City Police on April 30, 2015 and was held in lieu of $100,000 bond. She resigned from the Pell City School System at the time of arrest. She pleaded not guilty in June 2015.

On March 14, 2016, Gilley appeared before St. Clair County Circuit Court Judge Phillip Seay and entered a guilty plea to two counts of school employee engaging in a sex act with a student, one count per victim. Each count carried a Class B felony grade under the Alabama school-employee statute, which applies to any student under nineteen regardless of the general age of consent. Judge Seay imposed two concurrent ten-year terms. A probation hearing was scheduled for June 2016.

On June 17, 2016, Judge Seay denied probation, having heard the District Attorney's opposition that had also been on the record at the time of the guilty plea. Gilley apologized in court at the probation hearing. Her custodial sentence was set to begin June 20, 2016, and she surrendered her teaching license.

NOTE: One of the two victims was 18 — legally an adult under most general-purpose statutes — but the Alabama school-employee provision reaches any student under nineteen regardless of the broader age of consent. This is the analytical version of the institutional-trust rule encoded in statute, and the case is a useful exemplar for how the state-level law differs from age-of-consent framing alone. Contact_type 3 follows from the statute's requirement of sexual intercourse or deviant sexual intercourse. Probation was denied at a separate hearing rather than at sentencing, an unusual two-step disposition pattern; the DA's opposition at the time of plea is what positioned Gilley for the denial. Discovery vector was the school resource officer rather than a parent or victim — SRO-initiated investigation is a relatively uncommon discovery vector in the corpus.
