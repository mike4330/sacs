Amy Lynn Craft #847 sentenced

StateMississippiCourtDeSoto County Circuit Court, 17th Judicial District
JudgeunknownIncident Date2020-12-01
Defendant Age48Victim Age12
Prison Sentence12.5 yrs

Former teaching assistant at Overpark Elementary, Olive Branch, DeSoto County, Mississippi. Male victim Kohen Chamberlin, ages 12–13 during abuse. Married; two sons, one autistic. Active member of church community for 20-plus years. Undergoing cancer treatment at time of sentencing.

Abuse began December 2020. Discovered April 15, 2021 when a DeSoto County deputy on routine patrol spotted a car parked in front of Center Hill High School at approximately 1 a.m. and found Craft and the boy inside. Arrested the same day on one count of felony fondling. A second count was added April 20, 2021 after further investigation revealed additional evidence of inappropriate touching.

Victim impact statement delivered by father Christopher Chamberlin at sentencing: "Our child never anticipated that his innocence would be stripped away at the age of 12 and 13... The carefree mentality of an adolescent was ejected from our child due to the perversion of an adult." Father stated the agreed sentence was discussed with and accepted by the family.

Craft made a full allocution: expressed remorse to the victim and family; acknowledged fault; referenced her Christian faith and stated she had been in therapy since April 2021. Asked the court for mercy citing her children and cancer treatment.

Pleaded guilty to two counts of fondling (MS §97-5-23). Sentenced September 16, 2022 by Judge Gerald W. Chatham Sr.: Count 1 — 12.5 years MDOC, day for day; Count 2 — 15 years post-release supervision (5 years reporting, 10 non-reporting). $1,000 to Mississippi Children's Trust Fund; $1,000 to Victims of Human Trafficking and Commercial Sexual Exploitation Fund. No-contact order with victim and victim's family. Credit for 5 days served.

NOTE: Judge Chatham addressed Craft's stated Christian faith directly from the bench: "your internal life will be with Jesus, and to me that's the most important thing." Church members, friends, and family filled the defense side of the courtroom. The allocution and judicial remarks are among the most overtly religious in the DB. Case number CR-2021-897-GC(D).
