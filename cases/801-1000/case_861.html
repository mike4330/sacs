Former artistic director of Satori School for the Arts and owner of Satori Jazz Dance Company, Murphys, California. Age 31 at time of offense. Previously employed at Body Language Fitness in Arnold; terminated after hosting unauthorized parties with minors at her employer's home and providing them alcohol.

Victim was a 16-year-old male student at the dance studio. Sexual intercourse occurred at Davey's home June 5, 2010; the victim told his parents he would be babysitting for her that night. Davey disclosed the incident to a colleague at a rehearsal the following day, giggling as she described it as "something really bad." The colleague, Sean Lewis, organized a parent meeting at which Davey acknowledged the conduct but attributed it to loneliness. Davey agreed to turn herself in; investigation opened July 2010. Despite a prior pattern of hosting minors at private residences and providing alcohol, no charges were filed for approximately six months.

Arrested December 2010; charged with unlawful sexual intercourse with a minor (California PC § 261.5, felony). Pled not guilty December 23, 2010; Judge John E. Martin imposed a restraining order (150-yard exclusion from victim). Released on electronic monitoring but continued teaching minors at the studio in apparent violation of monitoring conditions. Pled guilty under a plea agreement providing for felony-to-misdemeanor reduction upon successful probation completion. Sentenced February 2011 by Judge Thomas A. Smith: 60 days jail, served entirely via ankle bracelet under the county's alternative sentencing program; felony probation with restrictions on unsupervised contact with males ages 10–17. Probation conditions required completion of sex offender treatment. After completing the first year of probation, the felony was reduced to a misdemeanor by Judge Martin in August 2012.

NOTE: sentence_years=0; 60-day sentence served entirely via ankle-bracelet alternative placement. Six-month delay between initial report (July 2010) and arrest (December 2010) drew substantial public criticism; community members, the victim's father, and a DA's colleague independently characterized the delay as gender-biased. Community was divided at sentencing — Davey's supporters physically blocked press photographers at court appearances. Victim's father opposed electronic monitoring credit and requested Davey's students be notified of her conviction.
