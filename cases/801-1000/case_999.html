Amy Gilly #999 sentenced

Former teacher at Hale Center High School, Hale Center ISD, Hale County, Texas. Age 46 at time of offense.

One male student victim, age 15. Gilly began communicating with the victim in early November 2022. On December 16, 2022, she drove him from a church to a location near train tracks on East 1st Street, where they kissed and she groped his genitals through his clothing. Text messages indicated she planned additional sexual contact later that week. Gilly gave the victim a gold necklace and other gifts in exchange for sexual favors. When questioned by police, Gilly admitted the relationship and stated she knew it was wrong and that she had feelings for the student.

School officials alerted Hale Center Police Department on December 16, 2022. Arrested that day; booked Hale County Detention Center, bond $20,000. A federal grand jury indicted Gilly in January 2023 on enticement and attempted enticement of a minor (carrying 10 years to life). In March 2023 she pleaded guilty to the lesser federal charge of transferring obscene material to a minor. No state indictment was returned. Sentenced July 20, 2023 by U.S. District Judge James Wesley Hendrix to 27 months federal prison (within the PSR-recommended range of 21–27 months). Teacher certificate relinquished. Defense: David Guinn. Federal prosecutor: Calandra Woolam.

Defense argued grief mitigation: defendant's husband died December 17, 2021 from COVID complications; preceded by deaths of a family member from Myeloma (2006), her mother from ALS complications (~2012), and her sister from liver and kidney failure (2020). Hendrix acknowledged the case differed from typical enticement patterns but found 27 months appropriate, noting the victim had a right to attend school without being viewed as a sexual object.

NOTE: Charge reduction from enticement (10 years–life) to obscene material transfer (up to 10 years) understates the contact conduct. PSR noted an upward variance was appropriate given uncharged sexual contact and the gift-for-sex arrangement. Defendant was widowed approximately 11 months before offense onset.
