Elementary school teacher in Coweta County, Georgia. Age 38 at the time of the documented offense conduct in early 2011; 39 at sentencing in February 2012. The known victim was a 14-year-old male; he was 13 when the conduct began.

The relationship was not a teacher-of-victim arrangement in the conventional sense. Schmieder had known the victim since his birth and had been a long-standing close family friend through a shared church congregation. The victim's mother described her as a "mother-figure" to her son, and the two families had been close for approximately ten years; the children were friends and the parents socialized together routinely.

The victim's parents began noticing a shift in the relationship between Schmieder and their son — she was behaving more like a peer than a parental figure. They opened the victim's Facebook page and discovered evidence of an inappropriate relationship. The investigation opened in April 2011 with a criminal referral to local authorities. The victim's mother delivered an emotional impact statement at sentencing describing the family's discovery, the experience of betrayal, and the recovered messages that progressively confirmed the abuse.

Schmieder was originally charged with aggravated child molestation under Georgia law. Coweta Judicial Circuit Senior Assistant District Attorney Kevin McMurry negotiated a plea reducing the charge to standard child molestation, which carried a 20-year statutory maximum per count rather than the aggravated charge's 25-year maximum. McMurry said publicly that he treated the case as he would have if the perpetrator had been male, and crafted the sentence to recognize the life-long impact on the victim.

On February 17, 2012, Coweta Superior Court Judge Jack Kirby imposed a 40-year sentence with 20 years to serve in state prison and the balance on probation. The press described it as the longest sentence handed down by a U.S. court on a female educator for underage sex up to that point; legal observers compared the term to those imposed for manslaughter and other serious felonies.

NOTE: The case sits at the boundary of the project's scope criterion. Schmieder's position as an elementary school teacher is mentioned in the press lead as the corpus hook, but the operative access vector documented in the record was the long-standing family-friend and quasi-mother-figure relationship rather than a teacher-of-victim arrangement — the victim was not a student in her elementary school. The prosecutor's framing at sentencing emphasized abuse of the family-trust position rather than abuse of the teacher role: "She abused her position and the trust bestowed on her from the family and she had to be punished for this." The falsification test under the school-employment criterion is therefore weaker for this case than for typical corpus members; the broader institutional-trust framing (teacher status as part of the trustworthy-adult profile that secured family confidence) is what positions the case in scope.

The 40-year imposed term and 20-year prison portion remain among the longest in the corpus and the case is one of the clearest instances of a prosecutor publicly naming a male/female sentencing-symmetry intent. The Facebook-page-discovery vector and the loaded "mother-figure" / "gift from God" maternal-trust framing in the victim impact statement are both worth retaining in the case record.
