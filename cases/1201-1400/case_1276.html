Former teacher and coach at King High School, part of the Corpus Christi Independent School District in Corpus Christi, Texas (Nueces County). Age 30 at the time of the documented offense; 31 at the time the second indictment was returned. Two known male victims, both 17 and both students at King High School; the second victim's age was not publicly released, and the recorded victim's age is preserved here as the case identifier.

The first documented incident occurred near the end of the 2013-14 school year. Ramirez exchanged more than 300 text messages with the victim leading up to the encounter, then had him driven to her home at 2 a.m. by a friend who waited outside while the encounter took place. The victim recorded the encounter on his cellphone and afterward shared the video with 11 of his friends. The King High School principal contacted police after the victim showed her the video. Investigators identified Ramirez through a distinctive eight-inch tattoo across her lower back visible in the recording.

Ramirez was fired by the district shortly after the allegations surfaced. She turned herself in to Corpus Christi police on August 4, 2014, was charged with one second-degree felony count of improper relationship between educator and student, and was released the next day on a $10,000 bond with GPS monitoring and no-minors-contact conditions; she was permitted to remain in her home with her ten-year-old child. The Texas Education Agency's Professional Discipline Unit opened a parallel certification investigation. Ramirez was first indicted in September 2014.

The trial was initially scheduled for September 2015. In the days before that setting, prosecutors offered a plea deal that would have required Ramirez to plead guilty and surrender her teaching license; she refused. On September 3, 2015, a grand jury returned a second second-degree felony indictment against her involving the second student, and the trial was reset. Defense attorney Amie Pratt told reporters Ramirez was 'dismayed at the additional, untrue allegations' and looked forward to her day in court. The defense theory in the original case was that the recorded victim was a legal consenting adult at the time (Texas's age of consent is 17) and was not Ramirez's personal student; defense counsel said publicly that Ramirez hoped to continue teaching after trial.

The case eventually resolved by negotiated plea on January 11, 2016: Ramirez pleaded guilty to count one and no contest to count two, and was sentenced to seven years probation plus a $4,000 fine. No prison time was served, and no sex-offender registration was required — Ramirez surrendered her teaching license in lieu of registration. The convictions were upheld on appeal on January 31, 2018.

NOTE: License-surrender-in-lieu-of-SOR is a disposition occasionally available under Texas law in educator cases and is worth flagging for cross-case comparison. Recorded sexual conduct with a 17-year-old is CSAM under federal law (18 U.S.C. § 2256) even though the conduct fell at Texas's age of consent. Defense theory tested whether the institutional connection extends beyond a defendant's 'personal' students — Texas's improper-relationship statute reaches any student in a public school district where the educator is employed, irrespective of direct teaching contact.
