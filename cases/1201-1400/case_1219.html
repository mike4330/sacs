Darcie Esson #1219 sentenced

StateColoradoCourtNinth Judicial District Court
JudgeDenise LynchIncident Date2006-10-01

Former teacher at Elizabeth High School, Front Range, Colorado. Male student victim, age 17 (high school football player).

Offense: Fall 2006 in Glenwood Springs - invited student to hotel room, offered alcohol, had sex with him twice on the floor while defendant's 4-year-old and 8-year-old daughters and 17-year-old babysitter slept in same room. Afterward pressured victim to lie and keep silent, called victim repeatedly emphasizing it was his responsibility to stay quiet.

Defendant: Darcie Esson, age 32-33 at offense. Mother of two young daughters (4 and 8). Going through divorce at time of offense.

Charged: Three counts of sexual assault on child (age 15-17) by person in position of trust, one with pattern of abuse allegation.

Pleaded guilty (June 2007) to one count; two counts dropped in plea agreement.

Sentenced (December 14, 2007): 90 days jail + 10 years to life supervised probation as sex offender. Additional: random urine tests, substance abuse/alcohol treatment, mental health evaluation, sex offender registration (within 5 days), sex offender-specific treatment, 7,000+ restitution.

Victim impact: Lost interest in school and athletics; depression, ran away from home; completed junior year via online schooling; required counseling. School administration threatened disciplinary action against victim even after disclosure. Peers ridiculed victim.

Defense claimed drunk (prosecutor noted she remembered other details). Defense emphasized remorse and impact on her children. Prosecutor sought 2 years to life, noting community corrections rejected her.

Judge Denise Lynch noted: Esson was 33-year-old woman in position of trust as teacher; she should have said no.

NOTE: Victim impact statement emphasized how offense changed victim's life trajectory. Pattern of grooming (alcohol provision, isolation in hotel room with sleeping children, post-assault manipulation and coercion to silence) indicates premeditation despite defense framing as isolated drunk act.
