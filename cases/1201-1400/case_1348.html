Swim coach, pool manager, and school counselor/therapist at Riverside High School, Riverside Beaver County School District, Beaver County, Pennsylvania (North Sewickley Township / Ellwood City area). Full name Alaina Mackenzie Marshall (born 1995). Age 27 at start of offense (2023); 29 at arrest (February 2025). Resident of Beaver Falls / Aliquippa area. Also worked as a contracted school therapist for Glade Run Lutheran Services (no longer employed there following the arrest). Defense attorney Stephen Domenic Colafella. Prosecutor Angela May Reed Strathman (assistant district attorney). Sentencing judge Kim Tesla.

Beginning in January 2023, Marshall engaged in a sexual relationship with a male member of the swim team she coached. The relationship continued through 2025, when the victim was 16 to 18 years old. Investigators said they had sex approximately 17 times during his sophomore and junior years and had sexual contact nearly every day the summer following his junior year in 2024. Known locations included the pool room at Riverside High School and Blackhawk High School following a swim meet; daily encounters also occurred during summer 2024 when Marshall worked as a lifeguard and the victim worked alongside her. The complaint states the relationship began after the student spoke with Marshall — who was also a therapist — about a breakup he was going through.

Arrested February 26, 2025 (MDJ docket MJ-36301-CR-0000066-2025; OTN F 1010666-6). Held overnight at Beaver County Prison and released February 27 after bail was modified from straight $250,000 monetary to 10 percent; Thomas Clark Marshall posted $25,000 cash. Early press and a Pennsylvania Department of Education certification notice described 34 counts of institutional sexual assault plus unlawful contact with a minor and corruption of minors. The Common Pleas criminal information and final disposition (CP-04-CR-0000441-2025) carry three counts of school intercourse/sexual contact with a student (third-degree felonies). Preliminary hearing waived March 21, 2025. The Pennsylvania Department of Education issued an immediate suspension of her certification on June 24, 2025. USA Swimming SafeSport listed a temporary suspension effective March 12, 2025.

Guilty plea November 19, 2025 before Judge Kim Tesla to all three counts. Sentenced March 20, 2026: aggregate 14 years probation (7 years on count 1 consecutive to 7 years on count 2; count 3 concurrent) plus 5 years electronic monitoring. Restrictive probation condition of 2.5 years work release at Beaver County Jail, with an alternate path to electronic monitoring in lieu of work release if fees were paid or indigency found within 10 days. Conditions include SORNA registration (notice of duty to register for 25 years issued March 20, 2026), sex-offender evaluation and treatment, mental-health evaluation and treatment, no contact with the victim, no teaching/coaching/therapy work involving minors, and monitored device/internet access only as approved by adult probation. No state-prison term was imposed. sentence_years = 0 (work-release/electronic-monitoring probation structure, not continuous prison).

Post-sentence: April 15, 2026 order converting SCRAM-X to standard GPS monitoring. On March 20, 2026 — the same day as sentencing and the start of her SORNA registration obligation — she incurred a separate registration-violation case (MJ-36302-CR-0000139-2026): failure to register with the Pennsylvania State Police and failure to provide accurate registration information. That complaint was filed May 29, 2026; bail set at $75,000, which she did not post. The sex-case docket shows confinement at Beaver County Prison known as of May 21, 2026, still in custody, and probation-violation hearings continued through mid-June 2026. Formal arraignment on the registration case was set for August 19, 2026; trial set for October 26, 2026.

NOTE: No-prison outcome on a multi-year institutional sexual assault pattern with roughly 17 documented sexual acts and near-daily summer contact — probation and electronic monitoring rather than state prison.
