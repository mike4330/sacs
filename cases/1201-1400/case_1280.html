Former physical education teacher at Hallsburg Elementary School, Hallsburg Independent School District, Hallsburg, McLennan County, Texas. Age 33 at time of offense (born approximately 1973–74; age 40 at November 2013 plea). Male victim, age 13, a student at Hallsburg Elementary during the 2006–2007 school year.

Rush was arrested in November 2007. A grand jury did not indict her until February 23, 2011 — three years and three months after arrest. The indictment carried 18 counts: five counts of aggravated sexual assault of a child, five counts of improper relationship between educator and student by deviate sexual intercourse, four counts of indecency with a child, and four counts of improper relationship between educator and student by sexual contact. The specific conduct underlying the ten oral sex counts was Rush performing oral sex on the victim. The case was initially filed as cause no. 2011-423-C1, then transferred to cause no. 2011-1857-C1 in August 2011. The Texas Attorney General's Office substituted as prosecutor because McLennan County DA Abel Reyna had previously represented Rush in private practice; AAG Thomas Cloudt handled the case.

The victim testified at trial to five incidents in early 2007: (1) February or early March 2007, Rush's kitchen; (2) days later, Rush's truck in the school gym parking lot; (3) March 2007, Rush's swimming pool; (4) Rush's shop, victim seated on an ATV; (5) Rush's shop while looking for a gas can.

At trial in December 2011 (19th District Court, cause no. 2011-1857-C1A, Judge Ralph Strother), the jury acquitted Rush on 10 counts and hung on 8. The trial court declared a mistrial on the 8 hung counts. Rush filed a pretrial writ of habeas corpus alleging double jeopardy barred retrial. The Tenth Court of Appeals affirmed denial of habeas relief on August 23, 2012 (No. 10-12-00119-CR; Chief Justice Gray, Justice Davis, Justice Scoggins), holding that no election was required because the jury heard no more offenses than counts in the indictment, and that the hung counts were plainly the first two oral sex incidents (counts 1–4). Retrial was authorized.

On November 15, 2013, Rush pleaded guilty to one count of injury to a child (recklessly causing mental or emotional injury, a non-sex charge) before Judge Strother. The AG's office agreed to dismiss the 8 remaining counts at sentencing. On January 13, 2014, Strother sentenced Rush to 8 years deferred probation, a $2,000 fine, and community service. Successful completion of deferred probation results in no conviction on her record. Teaching certificate permanently surrendered. No sex offender registration required. Defense attorney: Bill Johnston.

NOTE: The AG's substitution as prosecutor was explicitly caused by DA Abel Reyna's prior private representation of Rush — a textbook conflict of interest. Rush's husband, Waco police officer Ben Rush, publicly stated he had stood by his wife throughout the six-year case. The 3.5-year pre-indictment delay, cause number transfer, seven retrial resets after the hung jury, and ultimate non-sex deferred outcome are consistent with sustained institutional pressure on the prosecution from the defendant's law enforcement household connection. Defense attorney Johnston stated after the plea: "The only verdicts the jury returned were not guilty verdicts... This was not a sex offense." The full docket spans dozens of cause numbers with 100–200 entries each. Final conviction was for injury to a child rather than any sex offense — one of the most extreme charge reductions in the DB.
