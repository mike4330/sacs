Former softball coach at Cuyahoga Falls High School and physical-education teacher at Preston Elementary, Cuyahoga Falls City Schools, Summit County, Ohio. Age 32 at the time of the documented offense conduct in fall 2015; 33 at the time of the January 2016 reprimand and return to teaching; 42 at the eventual 2025 plea and sentencing. The known victim was a high-school student on the softball team Brown coached. Available reporting refers to the victim as a softball player without specifying gender; HS softball context typically implies a female victim, though the existing record had previously been coded as male — this point is flagged for verification when more sources surface.

The earliest documented incident is a Saturday, October 17, 2015 overnight stay at Brown's home. In a subsequent district investigation, Brown admitted to permitting the student to stay overnight, to a sustained pattern of text-message communication including messages sent after midnight, to addressing the student as 'babe' in texts, and to telling the student 'I love you' in other text messages. The conduct was reported to police in the fall of 2015.

The Cuyahoga Falls City Schools investigation concluded in early January 2016. Rather than terminate Brown, the superintendent issued a letter of reprimand citing failure to follow district policy and warning that further misconduct could lead to termination. Brown returned to teach the week of January 4, 2016, but was not returned to the softball coaching role. At the time of return, no criminal charges had been filed.

The criminal case proceeded years later, on a substantially delayed timeline. Brown was eventually charged with sexual battery in connection with the same conduct. In June 2025 she pleaded guilty to a reduced charge of endangering children; the sexual-battery count was dismissed at plea. She was sentenced in August 2025 to three years in the Ohio Department of Rehabilitation and Correction. A civil lawsuit was subsequently filed against Cuyahoga Falls City Schools, alleging that the district had ignored Brown's conduct.

NOTE: This case is a clean instance of the prior-warning-ignored pattern. The October 2015 conduct was reported to police, internally investigated, documented in writing through Brown's own admissions to the superintendent, and resulted in a reprimand and return-to-teaching outcome with no immediate criminal action. Approximately ten years separated the documented admissions from the eventual criminal disposition. The civil suit naming the district squarely raises the institutional-negligence question. Charge reduction at plea took the conviction down from sexual battery to a non-sex endangering-children count, which is the basis for contact_type = 888 on the conviction charge.
