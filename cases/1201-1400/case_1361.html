Former teacher at Nathanael Greene Academy, a private Christian school in Siloam, Georgia (Greene County). Age approximately 24 at time of offense (age 25 at arrest, age 26 at sentencing). The victim was a male student, age 15 at the time of the offense.

A Georgia Bureau of Investigation inquiry opened June 14, 2024 after the Greene County Sheriff's Office asked the GBI to investigate allegations of sexual contact between a student and Brown, then a teacher at Nathanael Greene Academy. Brown was arrested March 21, 2025. A second teacher from the same school, Sherri Delle Mauldin, was later identified as involved in similar conduct with the same student and was arrested March 24, 2025.

Brown was convicted at trial on five counts of improper sexual contact by an educator. Unlike Mauldin, who pleaded guilty and admitted the abuse, Brown maintained her innocence through trial. At sentencing on June 11, 2026, the victim — by then 19 years old — gave a victim impact statement in which he told Brown she should have admitted the abuse as Mauldin had, to spare him the pain of a trial; he became too overwhelmed to finish and had to be led from the courtroom hyperventilating, with an advocate completing the statement on his behalf. Brown declined to address the court. Her father asked the court for leniency, describing his daughter as having suffered violent illness, insomnia, and migraines since being charged.

Assistant District Attorney Blayne May prosecuted the case. Judge Stephen Bradley sentenced Brown to 40 years, with a minimum of 15 years to be served in prison, telling the court that individuals in positions of trust over children are expected to act in the children's interest. Nathanael Greene Academy has since closed permanently as a result of the scandal involving both teachers.

NOTE: This is one of two Nathanael Greene Academy teacher cases involving the same victim; see Sherri Delle Mauldin (co-case). Brown was the first of the two teachers arrested but was sentenced after Mauldin, following a trial rather than a plea. Brown's refusal to admit the conduct — contrasted with Mauldin's blind guilty plea — produced a markedly harsher sentence (40 years/15 mandatory vs. 12 years) and, per the victim's own statement, compounded his trauma by forcing him through a trial and having him publicly branded a liar during the interim. Before the offense, Brown had been a valedictorian and homecoming queen at the same school where she later taught.
