Former first-grade teacher at St. John Elementary School, St. John School District, St. John, Whitman County, Washington. Hired September 2025. Age 25 at time of offense. Married at the time of the offense.

Male victim, age 16, a student at the neighboring high school within the same small district. Investigators believe Naught met the victim outside her first-grade classroom role; criminal liability under Washington law rests on her status as a school employee and the victim's status as a student in the district. The two communicated via Snapchat. On at least one occasion the victim picked Naught up in his truck and drove to a remote location, where they had sexual intercourse. The victim told detectives that sexual contact occurred more than once off campus earlier in 2026. Naught asked the teen not to tell anyone and later apologized to him, saying she knew it was wrong.

The investigation began in May 2026 when Naught's husband, reviewing her cellphone amid marital problems, found text messages between her and the student and contacted the Whitman County Sheriff's Office. He also reported that she had confessed to him that she had sex with the 16-year-old "a couple of times." Deputies interviewed the husband and the teen; the accounts were consistent. Naught initially told investigators the two had only kissed, then admitted to intercourse. Location-tracking data corroborated the teen's account of the remote-location encounter.

Arrested May 10, 2026 at her St. John home and charged with first-degree sexual misconduct with a minor, a Class C felony. First appearance May 11 in Whitman County Superior Court; held on $10,000 bond. Arraigned May 15 before Judge Roger Sandberg and pleaded not guilty. Conditions included no contact with individuals under 18 and a sexual assault protection order for the victim. The St. John School District placed Naught on leave. Her husband filed for divorce shortly after the arrest.

Pleaded guilty July 10, 2026 and was remanded to the Whitman County Jail pending sentencing. Under the plea agreement she faces a standard range of six to twelve months and ten-year sex-offender registration; a five-year sexual assault protection order for the victim remains in place. Senior Deputy Prosecutor Tessa Scholl stated that the plea "spares [the victim] the burden of having to testify at trial." Sentencing is set for August 28, 2026.

NOTE: Washington's general age of consent is 16; liability here is the school-employee position-of-trust statute. Classic husband-reported pattern — discovery of phone evidence during marital conflict, followed by a spousal confession. Sentencing still pending as of early August 2026.
