Elizabeth M. Barthelmas #1302 sentenced

Former fifth-grade teacher at Dorothy Nolan Elementary School, Saratoga Springs, New York (Saratoga County). Resident of Wilton at the time of arrest. Age 38 at time of offense; 49 at sentencing.

Male victim age 11 at offense onset (a former fifth-grade student of hers; statutory threshold for the convicted charge requires a victim under 13). Offenses occurred between July and December 2007, after the victim had completed Barthelmas's 5th-grade class and was entering 6th grade.

Arrested by the Saratoga County Sheriff's Office in June 2017 — approximately ten years after the conduct, illustrating delayed disclosure consistent with elementary-age-victim cases. Pleaded guilty in July 2018 to one count of rape in the first degree (NY Penal Law 130.35; statutory subsection covers sexual intercourse with a victim under 13 by an adult offender 18 or older).

Sentenced September 5, 2018 by Saratoga County Court Judge James A. Murphy III to 15 years in prison followed by 20 years of post-release supervision; sex offender registration. An order of protection in favor of the victim was issued through 2041. Saratoga County District Attorney Karen A. Heggen prosecuted.

At sentencing the victim addressed Barthelmas directly from the stand: "You took away one of the most important aspects in my life, my innocence … You tainted a precious period of my youth and took something from me that I will never be able to get back … As a child, you took full advantage of my body and my brain. It's sickening." He also spoke to the stigma of male victimhood and the importance of holding female perpetrators accountable: "No man or woman should have to endure sexual abuse at any age. I hope my story will encourage other victims to share theirs. I will prove that good may come from evil. I'm a survivor."

Judge Murphy responded: Barthelmas "violated the ultimate trust a teacher has with their students."

NOTE: 10-year reporting delay, victim age 11 (5th-grade student), and 15-year prison sentence place this case at the high-severity end of the corpus and an elementary-school context that is comparatively rare here (most cases involve middle/high-school victims). The victim's articulated stand at sentencing addressing male-victim stigma and female-perpetrator accountability is unusually direct and is preserved verbatim above. Order of protection through 2041 implies a 23-year forward window.
