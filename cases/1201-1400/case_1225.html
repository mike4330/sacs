Melissa Diana Koehn #1225 sentenced

StateMinnesotaCourtDakota County District Court
JudgeRobert KingIncident Date2008-07-01
Defendant Age29Victim Age17
Prison Sentence0.4 yrs

Former history teacher and basketball coach at St. Croix Lutheran High School, West St. Paul, Minnesota (private school), Dakota County. Resident of Kenosha, Wisconsin. Age 29 at time of offense, 31 at sentencing.

Female victim, age 17. Koehn taught the victim history during her freshman year and coached her in basketball during sophomore year. Beginning in the victim’s sophomore year, Koehn initiated an informal mentoring relationship after the victim showed signs of depression. Koehn joined the family for dinners, holidays, social outings, and overnight visits; on nights she stayed, she slept in the victim’s bedroom with the parents’ knowledge and consent. An elaborate love letter was later found.

Koehn took a leave of absence in summer 2008. The relationship escalated to sexual contact beginning July 2008, shortly before the victim’s senior year. Conduct consisted of mutual breast touching and Koehn touching the victim’s vagina, described by Koehn as a “handful” of incidents, all in the victim’s bedroom in Apple Valley.

Discovered December 20, 2008, when an Apple Valley officer found the two kissing in a parked car; the officer later confirmed Koehn remained listed in the school staff directory as a teacher and coach on leave. Koehn was interviewed and arrested the following day, December 21, 2008.

Jury trial November 2009. The victim on the stand denied any sexual contact, then said she could not remember. Convicted November 20, 2009 of three counts of fourth-degree criminal sexual contact after three hours of deliberation. Sentenced February 5, 2010 by Judge Robert King: 3-year prison sentence stayed; 5 months county jail beginning February 11 plus 10 years probation; $200 fine; sex offender registration. Prosecutors had requested a prison sentence.

Defense argued the relationship was not criminal because Koehn was on leave when sexual conduct occurred. Prosecutors countered that she had taught and coached the victim during earlier school years and that she acknowledged still exercising authority over the victim.

NOTE: Private school. The institutional relationship through coaching and teaching predated and enabled the personal trust exploitation; sexual conduct began after Koehn went on leave but the jury found the prior authority relationship sufficient. Victim’s trial testimony (“can’t remember”) contrasts with her police statement, which named specific conduct. Lenient outcome — prosecutors sought prison; judge stayed the execution on a 3-year term.
