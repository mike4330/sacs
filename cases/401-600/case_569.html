Former PE teacher and basketball coach at Centertown Elementary School, McMinnville, Tennessee (Warren County). Born July 1, 1977; age 27 at time of offense. Married to Christopher Turner 2003–2005; they separated in 2004 and divorced during the offense period. Also known as Pamela Rogers Turner (married name at time of offense).

Sexually abused a 13-year-old male student over a three-month period beginning approximately November 2004. The relationship involved vaginal intercourse, oral sex (both forms), and digital penetration at the victim's home, her apartment, and the school gym — more than 12 occasions. Charged February 4, 2005 with 15 counts sexual battery by an authority figure and 13 counts statutory rape.

Pleaded no contest August 12, 2005 to four counts of sexual battery by an authority figure. Sentenced to 8 years suspended; to serve 270 days in Warren County jail, then 7 years 3 months probation. Ordered to surrender teaching certificate, register as sex offender for life, and barred from profiting from the case (books, movies, interviews) for 8 years.

Violated probation: arrested April 24, 2006 for sending nude photos, sex videos, and text messages to the same boy (age 15 at that point) using her father's cellphone, and maintaining contact through blogs and a website. Circuit Judge Bart Stanley revoked probation July 14, 2006 and ordered her to serve the remaining 7 years at the Debra K. Johnson Rehabilitation Center. Rogers apologized tearfully at the hearing; the judge stated she had done everything except show the court she wanted to rehabilitate herself.

Pleaded guilty January 10, 2007 to two counts of solicitation of sexual exploitation of a minor for sending the photos. Two additional years added to the existing sentence: 10-year total announced. A clinical psychologist testified she was a sex addict. Released approximately 2012.

NOTE: Post-release, Rogers was indicted May 28, 2015 for introduction of contraband into a state penal facility (conspiring to smuggle cell phones into prison where she had previously been incarcerated); sentenced to 8 more years. That offense is unrelated to educator abuse and is noted here for context only. This is one of the most widely covered female teacher cases in U.S. history — her appearance and the explicit nature of the photo/video evidence drove sustained national media attention.
