Courtney A. Jensen #561 sentenced

JudgeJames BablerIncident Date2014-09-01
Defendant Age24Victim Age17
Prison Sentence0.08 yrs

Former fifth-grade teacher and softball coach in the Cumberland School District, Cumberland, Wisconsin (Barron County). Age approximately 24 at start of offense (September 2014); 25 at arrest (May 2015). Resident of Cornell, Wisconsin.

Jensen began a romantic relationship with a 17-year-old female student she coached in softball in September 2014, starting with kissing and hugging; the relationship became sexual in February 2015. A school liaison officer was called to the school in May 2015 on a report of a teacher having sexual contact with a student. Jensen admitted the relationship and its progression when questioned. Placed on administrative leave; cooperated with the investigation.

Original charges: felony sexual assault of a student by staff, and misdemeanor sexual intercourse with a child age 16 or older. Per plea agreement, charges were reduced to five misdemeanor counts of fourth-degree sexual assault. Jensen surrendered her teaching license as a condition of the agreement.

Sentenced December 3, 2015 by Judge James Babler: 3 years probation, 30 days jail. DA Angela Beranek and defense attorney Sarah Mae Harless agreed not to recommend sex offender registration; the court did not impose it. Beranek recommended 6 months jail; judge imposed 30 days. Defense requested expungement of convictions after probation completion; DA argued against it, stating it would 'destroy the paper trail.'

At sentencing, the victim — by then 18, still in an ongoing relationship with Jensen — submitted a letter to Judge Babler asking him not to 'tear us apart,' writing that she had not been forced into the relationship and that 'the timing... was not the best because of the title she held.' A psychiatrist's report submitted by the defense noted Jensen used rationalizations and justifications and 'didn't fully appreciate the gravity of the offense, saying that the student wanted it and liked it.'

NOTE: Same-sex case; DA Beranek explicitly stated 'this was sexual exploitation and no less serious because it was two females.' The victim's active advocacy at sentencing — and the continuing relationship — is unusual in this DB. Charge reduction from felony to five misdemeanor counts significantly reduced sentencing exposure. The no-registration agreement between DA and defense is notable given the felony-level underlying conduct.
