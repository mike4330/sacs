Former paraprofessional and teacher's aide in the behavioral disabilities program at Appleton West High School, Appleton, Wisconsin (Outagamie County). Age 44 at arrest. Previously known as Laura Bates (married name); reverted to maiden name Ernst after divorce. Resident of Hilbert, later Brillion, Wisconsin.

The 17-year-old male special education victim had worked with Bates/Ernst in the behavioral disabilities program for approximately three years. Bates cultivated a friendship with the victim's mother through school contact, gaining access to the family home; the victim was allowed to spend time with Bates' family and do yard work at her Hilbert residence. Bates drove him home when his grandfather fell ill. Victim's mother later described hindsight signs of an inappropriate relationship.

In late October 2013, the victim came to her home and they kissed. The relationship became sexual in early November 2013: intercourse at her Harrison/Hilbert residence (Calumet County). On November 30, Bates accompanied the victim's mother to bars in Appleton, then slept over at the victim's Kaukauna home, where she and the victim had sex in the basement. She had also performed oral sex on a hiking trail near the Kaukauna Environmental Center earlier that month (Outagamie County incidents).

Investigation began November 30, 2013, after a Bates relative reported to school administrators that Bates said she 'could not live without the boy' and had ignored warnings that a physical relationship was inappropriate. Police interviewed Bates at the school; she admitted the relationship, describing the two as 'soul mates' and 'crazy about each other.' She resigned December 2, 2013.

Originally charged in Calumet County (incidents at her home) and Outagamie County (Kaukauna incidents). Per plea agreement, charges consolidated into Calumet County; Outagamie County charges dropped. Sentenced April 9, 2015 by visiting Sheboygan County Judge Angela Sutkiewicz: 1 year in jail with work-release for employment, counseling, and child care; 5 years probation; sex offender registration required. Substitute teaching and paraprofessional licenses previously revoked. Prosecutor: District Attorney Nicholas Bolz; defense: Kevin Musolf.

NOTE: Three-year proximity to the victim through the behavioral disabilities program is the primary access vector. The victim's mother's friendship with Bates, and the family's trust allowing home visits, was integral to the grooming pattern. Work-release converts the 1-year jail term to a nighttime-only sentence. Special education / behavioral disabilities classification of the victim is significant — an explicitly vulnerable population. Bates' statement to a family member ('could not live without' the student) is what triggered the report.
