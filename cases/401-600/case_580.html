Bridgett Szychulski #580 sentenced

Former band teacher at Lenape Middle School, Doylestown area (Central Bucks School District), Bucks County, Pennsylvania; subsequently transferred to Central Bucks High School South in Warrington. Age approximately 28–29 at time of offense in 2012; 32 at sentencing. Married with two children; taught in Central Bucks district seven years. Resident of Philadelphia.

Engaged in a sexual relationship with a 14-year-old male student over approximately six months in 2012. Had sexual intercourse with the boy at least three times — once in her car and twice at Lenape Middle School (in her office and in a storage room near the band room). Also sent him sexually explicit pictures of herself and lewd text messages.

The investigation began in September 2014 after an anonymous tip to a child abuse hotline. Szychulski was suspended and surrendered to police in October 2014. Arraigned on charges of deviate sexual intercourse, statutory sexual assault, aggravated indecent assault, and indecent assault; bail set at $500,000; ordered to have no contact with minors or students. She was pregnant at the time of arrest.

Pleaded guilty January 29, 2015 to statutory sexual assault and aggravated indecent assault on a minor. Sentencing deferred 90 days pending sexually violent predator assessment.

Sentenced June 24, 2015 by Judge Bateman: 11.5 to 23 months Bucks County prison; 8 years probation upon release; 200 hours community service; Megan's Law sex offender registration (name, address, photo on PA database); not classified as a sexually violent offender. Sentence was below the 2–3 year range cited by prosecution; judge stated what 'tipped the scales' was a letter from the victim himself requesting psychiatric treatment for Szychulski instead of a lengthy prison term.

At sentencing, Szychulski was accompanied by at least 37 friends and family members. She apologized to the victim's family, called her actions 'extremely selfish,' and said 'it makes me sick to think how he sees me now as someone who betrayed his trust.' Defense noted her husband had left her after the offense and that she was undergoing therapy. Defense cited emotional vulnerability due to marriage difficulties and a miscarriage at the time.

Prosecutor Lannetti noted Szychulski initially denied the charges when they came to light, characterizing that as revictimization of the boy. Victim had given up his music studies as a result of the offense.

NOTE: Victim actively advocated for a non-prison outcome in his letter to the court — one of the documented cases where victim's own request shaped the sentence downward. On-campus conduct (office and storage room). Two children ages 6 months and 4 years at sentencing (newborn at time of arrest).
