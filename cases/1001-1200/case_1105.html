Jessica Heather Defoor #1105 sentenced

JudgeunknownIncident Date2006-02-01

Former second-grade teacher at Danville Neel Elementary School, Morgan County Board of Education, Danville, Morgan County, Alabama. Age 32 at time of offense.

One female victim, age 14, the younger sister of Zachary Mullins (age 18). Defoor's access to the child was through her relationship with Mullins. In February 2006, Defoor brought the victim to her home, provided alcohol, and subjected her to sexual contact. Defoor invited the victim into her bedroom and attempted to engage her in further sexual contact, which the victim declined. Mullins was present; Defoor had sexual intercourse with Mullins in the same room as the child. Defoor had also taken the victim to concerts and provided alcohol on other occasions. Offense discovered when the victim disclosed to a teacher; school officials reported to authorities.

Alabama AG's Family Protection Unit presented evidence to a Morgan County grand jury, resulting in indictment July 23, 2009. Defoor was placed on paid administrative leave in approximately September 2007 (34 months before July 2010); she received 22,876 in salary during that period while awaiting termination proceedings. Jury convicted Defoor February 11, 2010. Sentenced March 30, 2010: six years for enticing a child for sexual purposes (Class C felony) and 12 months for second-degree sexual abuse (Class A misdemeanor), concurrent. Ordered to serve two years in prison; remaining four years subject to activation on probation violation; five years supervised probation; sex offender registration required. Defoor apologized in court and announced intent to appeal; granted a 0,000 appeal bond. Prosecution led by AAGs Andrew Arrington, Kelly Hawkins, and Stephanie Billingslea.

Co-defendant Zachary Mullins (the victim's brother) pleaded guilty to second-degree rape and second-degree sodomy for his conduct with his sister; sentenced to 10 years concurrent (3 years to serve, 7 years probation).

NOTE: Prosecution was by the Alabama AG's Family Protection Unit, not the local DA. Access to the victim was through the sibling relationship with Mullins, not directly through classroom access — Defoor's school role provided community credibility rather than direct student access. Defoor collected nearly 23,000 in salary during 34 months of paid administrative leave before her employment was formally resolved.
