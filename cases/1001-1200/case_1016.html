Former teacher at Cypress Lakes High School in Cypress-Fairbanks Independent School District (Katy, Harris County, Texas). Age 26 at the time of the documented offense conduct. The known victim was a 16-year-old male student enrolled at Cypress Lakes.

On or about April 3, 2014, Thompson had sexual intercourse with the victim in her vehicle parked outside his residence. The victim subsequently confirmed the relationship to investigators on May 20, 2014, stating that the last instance of intercourse had occurred prior to April 2, 2014 — while Thompson was still employed at the school.

The case opened on April 2, 2014, when an anonymous tip identifying Thompson and the victim by name was made to the Cypress-Fairbanks ISD tip line. When school administrators confronted Thompson, she identified the tipster to administrators by name and then sent the tipster multiple text messages urging her to deny knowledge of the relationship. Thompson also contacted a separate mutual friend, warning her that the tipster had been named to administrators and expressing concern about what the tipster might disclose. That conduct — coaching two of her own social contacts to suppress evidence after being confronted — formed the factual basis for the witness-tampering charge that the State filed alongside the educator-relationship charge.

The complaint was filed on May 20, 2014; bond was set at $10,000. Defense counsel John Michael Pettruzzi and Mauro Beltramini were retained.

On November 4, 2014, Thompson waived indictment and pleaded to deferred adjudication on the educator-relationship count: six years of community supervision. The witness-tampering count was dismissed simultaneously. She was briefly jailed on November 9-10, 2014. A motion for early termination was filed in August 2019 with additional orders entered in December 2019, and Thompson was discharged from supervision on November 9, 2020 — completion of the standard six-year deferred-adjudication term.

The witness-tampering count is the case's most distinctive feature: Thompson's post-confrontation outreach to her own social contacts to coach them on what to say is unusually direct concealment conduct, captured in text-message evidence. The dismissal of that count at plea is consistent with the corpus pattern of concealment counts being used as plea-leverage rather than as stand-alone convictions, although in some other corpus cases (cf.
