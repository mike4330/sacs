Former special education aide at Klondike Elementary School, Tippecanoe School Corporation, Lafayette, Tippecanoe County, Indiana. Age 35 at time of offense onset.

Male victim, age 9 when abuse began (4th grade) through age 14 (8th grade). Holmgren met the victim as his aide at Klondike Elementary and became a family fixture — tutoring both the victim and his sister, and running errands for the immigrant family who spoke limited English. Judge Meyer stated at sentencing that she 'worked your way into that family' and 'took advantage of that.' Weekly sexual abuse including oral sex performed on the victim, from 4th grade through 8th grade — approximately five years. Holmgren threatened the victim that bad things would happen to him and his family if he disclosed; Meyer characterized this as 'dangerous head games.' An email from Holmgren to the victim read: 'I don't care that you're still growing. I find you damn sexy.'

Discovered November 2019 when school officials at the victim's subsequent school became aware of a suspicious email between Holmgren and the student; law enforcement immediately notified. Holmgren was fired after an internal investigation and arrested November 8, 2019.

Tried by jury in late May 2021. Convicted of 1 count Level 1 felony child molesting and 1 count Level 4 felony child molesting, plus 1 count inappropriate communication with a child (1 year concurrent). Jury deadlocked on one additional Level 1 felony child molesting count and one Level 2 felony child seduction count; both dismissed by prosecution before sentencing. Sentenced November 2021 by Tippecanoe Superior 2 Judge Steve Meyer to 42 years (47 years total minus 5 suspended to probation). Good-time credit restricted to 85% rather than 75% due to victim age. Five years supervised probation upon release; lifetime sex offender registration. Deputy Prosecutor: Jackie Starbuck.

On appeal (Court of Appeals of Indiana No. 21A-CR-2756, September 30, 2022, Judge Tavitas), Holmgren challenged her credit-restricted felon classification and her Count II sentence under the enhanced sentencing provision, which applies when the victim is under twelve and carries a 20-to-50-year range rather than the standard 20-to-40-year range under subsection 4(b). The court upheld the credit-restricted felon designation — Indiana statute assigns that determination to the trial court, not the jury. The court reversed the Count II sentence on Sixth Amendment grounds: the jury was charged only with finding the victim under fourteen; the trial court's judicial finding that the victim was under twelve — which exposed Holmgren to the enhanced 4(c) range — violated Apprendi v. New Jersey. Remanded for resentencing of Count II, with the State given the option to retry the under-twelve question before a jury or resentence under subsection 4(b). On July 22, 2024, Holmgren was resentenced on Count II; Indiana DOC reflects a total sentence of 34 years.
