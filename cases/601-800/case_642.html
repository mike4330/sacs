Former teacher at Northfield Middle School (Northfield Public Schools), Northfield, Minnesota (Rice County). Age approximately 36–37 at time of offense; age 38 at sentencing.

Sent nude photographs of herself to multiple male students — estimated approximately a dozen boys — via Snapchat. The youngest recipient was an 8th grade student (approximately age 13; victim_age is inferred). Some photographs were taken from inside the school building. Boys sent photos back. She offered at least one student edibles. She stated she sent the photos because she liked the attention. She also invited at least one student to her home; he declined.

In March 2024, a student reported the conduct to Gabriel Crombie (33), the school resource officer assigned to Northfield schools. Crombie 'cornered' two students, who denied having proof. One student pulled out nude images of Hanson to show Crombie; Crombie photographed approximately five to six of the images on his personal cell phone and told the student to delete the originals. Crombie did not file a report, citing high daily report volume. In April 2024, a teacher reported the conduct; Crombie confronted Hanson on April 25, 2024 and she admitted 'everything.' Crombie told Hanson he would 'do her a favor' and 'let the case go.' The two subsequently began a dating relationship. No documentation of either report was found by police.

A student came forward in December 2024 with photos still on her phone. Hanson was placed on administrative leave December 9 and resigned December 11, 2024. Charges filed in Rice County in May 2025: Hanson on two felony counts of sending sexually explicit electronic communications to a minor; Crombie on a felony count of aiding an offender and a gross misdemeanor count of misconduct.

Hanson pleaded guilty to one count of engaging in electronic communication relating or describing sexual conduct with a child. Sentenced February 3, 2026: 60 days of electronic home monitoring (house arrest), five years of probation, 200 hours of community service. Probation violation would result in one year jail. No prison time.

NOTE: Non-contact offense — all conduct was digital (Snapchat). Headline describes victims as 'high school students' but charging documents confirm the youngest was an 8th grader (middle school). Approximately 8-month gap between initial suppressed report (March 2024) and actual investigation (December 2024) due to SRO cover-up. Crombie's conduct — photographing victim evidence on his personal phone and initiating a relationship with the offender — represents a compounded institutional failure. Superintendent Matt Hillmann publicly acknowledged the breakdown.
