Former physical education teacher and coach at Bridgewater-Raritan High School, Bridgewater, New Jersey (Somerset County). Taught health and physical education, driver education, and students with disabilities. Also coached field hockey and girls lacrosse. Age approximately 26 at time of first sexual offense (2011).

Victim was a female student, age 14-15 at onset of sexual conduct, who described herself as "shy and vulnerable." The relationship began in 2011 and turned sexual when the victim was 14-15; it continued through her senior year of high school (approximately 2014-2015). Grooming took place primarily on school grounds: Pinto gave the student alcohol, took her out for ice cream, and brought her to her home in Raritan Borough where they watched "The Real L Word," a Showtime series with lesbian themes. The physical sexual conduct occurred at Pinto's Raritan Borough home.

The case was initiated on October 16, 2023 when the Somerset County Prosecutor's Office Sex Crimes/Child Abuse Unit received an anonymous email from the Bridgewater Police Department alleging an inappropriate teacher-student relationship. On October 26, 2023, detectives interviewed the victim, now an adult, who reported the relationship and its timeline. Pinto was arrested November 8, 2023 in Franklin by Somerset County Prosecutor detectives and the Raritan Borough Police Department. Prosecutor: John McDonald (Somerset County). Chief of County Detectives Francisco Roman Jr. Raritan Borough Police Chief Raymond Nolte.

Charged November 2023 with first-degree aggravated sexual assault and two counts of second-degree sexual assault. Pleaded guilty November 6, 2024 to one count of sexual assault before Judge Peter Tober. Sentenced February 14, 2025 to 364 days Somerset County Jail. Pinto surrendered teaching certificates for health and physical education, driver education, and students with disabilities; the State Board of Examiners accepted the surrender in April 2025.

The victim filed a civil lawsuit against the Bridgewater-Raritan School District alleging the abuse was a "classic textbook case of the sexual grooming of a child by an adult authority figure," with most grooming and emotional abuse occurring on school grounds.
