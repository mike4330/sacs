Former English teacher at Atlantic Coast High School in Jacksonville, Florida (Duval County), employed by the Duval County School Board. Age 22 at the time of the documented offense; arrested days before her 23rd birthday. The known victim was a 16-year-old male student in her English class. The arrest report flagged a second 16-year-old male as a potential additional victim, but no separate charges were filed and no further public reporting on that individual is in available sources.

The sexual relationship spanned approximately three and a half months, from March 1, 2012 through June 15, 2012, and took place at Reed's residence rather than on school grounds. The victim told investigators that Reed performed oral sex on him on more than one occasion and that they had penile-vaginal sex on more than one occasion. To circumvent parental controls, Reed and the victim devised a cover story in which Reed posed as the mother of a fabricated friend named "Josh"; she drove to the victim's home with her chocolate Labrador "Dexter" and met the victim's mother, who allowed the victim to leave with her on the understanding that she was Josh's mother. The victim disclosed the relationship to his mother several months later, and the mother subsequently reported the matter to the school.

Reed was arrested on Friday, November 9, 2012 at 15:45 by Detective M.J. Collins of the Jacksonville Sheriff's Office and booked into the Pre-Trial Detention Facility. After being read her Miranda rights she answered preliminary questions, confirmed her teaching role, and then invoked counsel. She was charged with two F1 felony counts of sexual battery on a victim aged 12 to 17 with custodial authority — Count 1 for penile/vaginal contact, Count 2 for sodomy (oral and anal). Bond was set at $75,003 per count. At her initial appearance on November 10, 2012 before Judge Dawn Hudson, with State Attorney representative Brett Mereness present and retained defense counsel Don Mairs (formal name Donald B. Mairs), probable cause was filed; the indigent-status and public-defender boxes on the docket were struck through. Reed was released on her own recognizance on November 15, 2012 with conditions barring contact with the victim, contact with any school, and unsupervised contact with any minor, and requiring her to reside with her family in St. Johns County under a 9 p.m. curfew.

On November 29, 2012 the State Attorney's Office filed an information substantially restructuring the case. Count 1 was reduced from sexual battery to a single F3 felony count of child abuse — an intentional act expected to result in physical or mental injury. Count 2 was dropped. Reed pleaded guilty in open court before Duval County Circuit Judge Charles W. Arnold; adjudication was withheld, meaning no formal conviction was entered under Florida law. The court imposed five years of probation with conditions including no direct or indirect contact with the victim, no unsupervised contact with any child under 18, no employment or volunteering where children congregate, surrender of her teaching certificate, payment of all victim counseling and related expenses, and drug and mental-health evaluations. Defense counsel was Donald B. Mairs (private retained); the case was prosecuted by Theresa Elizabeth Simak.

NOTE: Significant charge reduction — two F1 sexual-battery counts (one penile-vaginal, one sodomy oral and anal, with penetrative conduct admitted by the victim and corroborated through the cover-story details) resolved to a single F3 child-abuse count with adjudication withheld. The contact_type on the conviction count is left null because the child-abuse statute is generic and does not specify a sexual act, but the underlying conduct documented in the arrest report supports a contact_type of 3 for analytical purposes. Adjudication-withheld dispositions in Florida produce no formal conviction; this pattern is comparable to Texas deferred-adjudication outcomes (cf. Sotelo #812). The cover-story / fake-friend / dog-as-prop deception aimed at the victim's mother is a distinctive concealment pattern. A second potential 16-year-old male victim was identified on the original arrest report but did not appear in the eventual charging instrument.
