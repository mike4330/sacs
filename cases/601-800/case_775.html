Sandy L. Binkley #775 sentenced

Former teacher and volleyball coach at Portland High School, Portland, Tennessee (Sumner County). Age 35 at time of offense. Married, mother of three (twin daughters). Also known as Sandy Leanne McCormack.

Victim was a 17-year-old male student. Sexual activity — oral sex and sexual intercourse — took place in the volleyball locker room at the school, spanning March through September 23, 2008. Binkley also purchased alcohol for the victim and three additional teenage boys at a market near the state line at Highways 109 and 31W.

A journal found in the closet of Binkley's classroom contained suggestive messages from students referring to her as "Sexy Sandy." The court kept the journal from the jury during trial but Judge Gay admitted it at sentencing, finding it relevant that she had kept it for years.

Binkley accused the victim of raping her — a counter-claim that deeply affected the victim. In his impact statement he described being labeled a rapist, feeling placed in a "lower class of people," and his 8-year-old brother getting into a fight at school defending the family name. The victim's mother testified to the family's emotional and financial toll.

Originally charged with 10 counts; convicted at trial September 29, 2009 on two counts of statutory rape by an authority figure (TCA § 39-13-532), acquitted on the rest. Sentenced November 19, 2009 by Criminal Court Judge Dee David Gay: 12 years (6 years per count, consecutive — the maximum). Parole eligible mid-2013. DA Ray Whitley; ADA Sallie Wade Brown prosecuted. Defense attorney David G. Ridings called the consecutive sentences "abuse."

Judge Gay: "Without truth you can't have justice; without truth you can't have rehabilitation. The truth has been very clear to me and the jury, and something the defendant has not embraced. I don't see any potential for rehabilitation."

Defense forensic psychologist Sandra Phillips testified Binkley had depression dating to 2001, childhood trauma (witnessed father beating mother daily), and emotional distress over her grandfather's declining health. Phillips assessed her as low risk to the community. Binkley took the stand and apologized but continued to blame the school administration.

Released July 18, 2017 — approximately 4 years early due to good behavior. Relocated to Auburn, Kentucky (Logan County); TBI registry status inactive (moved to another state).

NOTE: Maximum sentence case. Judge explicitly found "no potential for rehabilitation" due to defendant's refusal to accept responsibility. Binkley's counter-accusation of rape against the victim is a striking example of victim-blaming — the victim himself said the "rapist" label ruined his social life. The classroom journal suggests broader boundary issues beyond the single victim. Ridings noted she was charged with 10 counts and acquitted of almost everything, yet the judge ran the two convictions consecutively. contact_type = 3 (oral sex and sexual intercourse confirmed by judge at sentencing).
