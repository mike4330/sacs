Former 8th grade language arts teacher at William James Middle School, Bulloch County Schools, Georgia (Bulloch County). Age 35 at arrest (July 27, 2011). Had been teaching for 13 years; worked in Bulloch County for three years, previously employed by Candler County schools.

Victim was a 14-year-old male student in her class. Jackson used social networks and cell phones to initiate and maintain contact outside the classroom. She had sexual intercourse with the boy at his home while his parents were away. She also sent him sexually explicit photographs of herself and sexually graphic messages. No incidents occurred on school property. Jackson resigned before her arrest after learning it was pending.

The victim's parents filed a complaint on July 13, 2011. After approximately two weeks of investigation, Jackson was arrested July 27, 2011. Original charges included aggravated child molestation, three counts of child molestation, statutory rape, enticing a child for indecent purposes, sexual assault by a person with supervisory or disciplinary authority, two counts of computer or electronic child exploitation, sexual exploitation of children, and six counts of furnishing harmful material to minors.

On February 15, 2012, Jackson pleaded guilty to three counts: statutory rape, sexual exploitation of children, and unlawful disposition of sexually explicit material to minors. DA Richard Mallard nolle prossed the remaining five charges. Bulloch County Superior Court Judge Gates Peed sentenced her to 15 years for each felony, running concurrently (12 years for the third count), with five years to serve on the statutory rape count. The five years were then suspended in lieu of 500 to 600 days (maximum "year and eight months") in a women's probation detention center, where she was to receive a psychosexual evaluation. Additional conditions: 15 years probation, no contact with the victim or his family, surrender all teaching certificates, compliance with sex offender requirements, and a ban from the Ogeechee Judicial Circuit (Bulloch, Effingham, Jenkins, and Screven counties). Judge Peed accepted the plea "reluctantly," noting that had the sexes been reversed, the sentence would not have been in question.
