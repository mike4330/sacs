Former school counselor at Millcreek High School, St. George, Washington County School District, Utah. Age approximately 40 at time of offense (June 2008); 50 at sentencing (August 2018). Also held position as student success director at Dixie State University (February–October 2016); teaching license revoked 2016.

Victim: female, age 17, student at Millcreek High School who sought counseling from Tasso. Offense: June 2008 — Tasso kissed and touched the victim at Tasso's home in St. George (single charged incident). Victim (A.R.) reported in June 2016, approximately 8 years after the fact. Victim's sentencing statement characterized Tasso as manipulative and called her 'a grown woman who sexually assaults underage girls' who 'fooled' many people; described the offense as a betrayal of trust by someone the victim was particularly vulnerable with due to 'unfortunate history prior to contact.' Deputy County Attorney Shaum described the impact as significant, citing the victim's particular vulnerability.

At least two additional former students made complaints; not prosecuted due to statutes of limitations. Washington County School District had separately been notified in 2015 of an incident 'that apparently took place between 2002 and 2004' (different from the 2008 charged offense); Tasso resigned nine days after the district interview and has not worked for WCSD since.

Case filed June 2016; charges formally filed January 2018 in Fifth District Court (Deputy County Attorney Ryan Shaum; defense attorney Douglas Terry). Pleaded guilty June 2018 to one count unlawful sexual activity with a minor (third-degree felony). Sentenced August 9, 2018: 120 days Washington County Jail (reporting August 10); 36 months supervised private probation; 200 hours community service; National Sex Offender Registry registration. No prison time (per plea deal recommendation).

NOTE: Female victim in counselor-student context — victim sought mental health support; betrayal of therapeutic trust. Delayed reporting (8 years). At least 2 additional victims; statute of limitations precluded prosecution. District had received an earlier complaint about 2002-2004 conduct — institutional prior warning.
