Former teacher at Brazoswood High School in Brazosport Independent School District (Clute, Texas, Brazoria County). A former Teacher of the Year recipient at the school. Defendant's precise age at the time of the offense conduct is not stated in available sources; she was still employed at Brazoswood at the time of her September 2023 arrest, approximately seventeen years after the documented offense conduct. The known victim was a 15-year-old female student.

The relationship occurred off campus over approximately two years beginning in 2006. Because the offense pre-dated Texas's 2007 improper-relationship-between-educator-and-student statute, the State could not bring the educator-specific charge and instead charged the conduct under the broader sexual-assault-of-a-child and indecency-with-a-child statutes.

The case opened in September 2023 when the victim, then an adult, walked into the Clute Police Department and reported the offense — approximately seventeen years after it occurred. Clute Police Chief James Fitch stated that witness testimony was critical to the investigation given the elapsed time, and detectives tracked down former classmates and individuals to whom the victim had made initial outcries at the time of the conduct. Brazosport ISD placed Hargrove on administrative leave in September 2023 upon notification and terminated her employment in early 2024.

A Brazoria County grand jury indicted Hargrove in December 2023 on twelve counts: six counts of sexual assault of a child, four counts of indecency with a child by contact, and two counts of indecency with a child by exposure. Bond was set at $240,000.

On August 27, 2025, Hargrove pleaded guilty to all twelve counts. The court imposed ten years of probation with a ten-year suspended prison term on each count; no period of incarceration was imposed. Lifetime sex-offender registration was required, per Chief Fitch's post-sentencing statement. The victim spoke at the sentencing hearing. The Brazoria County District Attorney's Office declined to comment to the press.

NOTE: The seventeen-year gap between offense conduct and victim disclosure is among the longest delayed-reporting cases in the corpus. Chief Fitch noted publicly that the victim had had hesitations for years before coming forward; the SBI-style investigative reconstruction depended on locating contemporaneous outcry witnesses rather than physical evidence. The no-prison outcome on twelve felony counts — including six sexual-assault counts whose combined statutory maximum exposure exceeds one hundred years — is analytically significant. The pre-2007-statute charging structure is also notable: the case had to be brought under the broader child-victim statutes rather than the educator-specific statute that would have governed had the conduct occurred even one year later.
