Former substitute teacher at Mitchell High School (and occasionally Seven Springs Middle School), New Port Richey, Florida (Pasco County). Age 40 at arrest; 41 at sentencing. Married, mother of two.

Victim: 16-year-old male student at Mitchell High School. Grooming vector was unusual: Marinelli initially told the student he was a candidate boyfriend for her 15-year-old daughter, establishing social contact under a plausible pretext. After telling him he was "on the list," she began inviting him to her home. The relationship turned physical when she drove him to a location in the nearby Longleaf subdivision and they made out; Marinelli said repeatedly, "This is bad," and that she should not be doing it. They began seeing each other regularly after that. In early January 2008, Marinelli and the student had sex at her home; approximately ten more encounters followed.

Discovered February 2008 when the victim's father saw his son getting out of Marinelli's car pulling up his pants (both were in the backseat). The father reported to the Pasco County Sheriff's Office and turned over records of hundreds of cell phone calls and text messages. Messages included Marinelli writing, "U can meet me at home at 3. Real men only need 20 min!" and "How about a quickie 2 tomorrow afternoon?"

Marinelli had attended two days of substitute training and worked 113 days as a substitute at Mitchell High and Seven Springs Middle before her arrest. Her background check turned up no criminal record. The school district removed her from the substitute list once it learned of the investigation.

Arrested March 24, 2008 on charges of unlawful sex with a minor. Faced a maximum of 15 years and a sentencing guidelines minimum of 6 years.

Pleaded guilty April 18, 2009. No prison time. Sentenced to 1 year house arrest, no contact with minors under 18 without supervision, sex offender registration, STD testing, sex offender treatment, and residency restrictions (no living within 1,000 feet of a school or children's gathering place, excluding her own neighborhood bus stop). Defense attorney Kevin Piccarreto. Prosecutor Eva Vergos stated the state agreed to no prison to spare the victim — now 18 at sentencing — from being deposed and required to appear at trial.
