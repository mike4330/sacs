Stephanie Amato #221 sentenced

Former special education teacher at Ethan Allen Elementary School, Mayfair, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Female defendant, age 30 at arrest; single mother of a 7-month-old son at sentencing. One male student victim, age 14, in her special education class. Six years employed in the Philadelphia School District; Temple University magna cum laude; master's degree from Cabrini University with 4.0 GPA.

Sexual relationship with the victim occurred March–April 2014 across multiple locations: her car, her parents' home in Northeast Philadelphia, and a park. Incidents began while Amato was actively using heroin daily (having relapsed after two inpatient treatment attempts in 2013–2014). DNA testing of her parents' sofa and car seats confirmed the sexual relationship. During the school's investigation, Amato maintained contact with the victim and warned him: 'You know I can get arrested if you tell.' The victim eventually disclosed.

Arrested June 4, 2014; turned herself in to police; released on $125,000 bail. Original charges: Involuntary Deviate Sexual Intercourse, Statutory Sexual Assault, Unlawful Contact with a Minor, Interference with Custody of Children, Endangering the Welfare of a Child, Corruption of a Minor, Indecent Assault, and Indecent Exposure.

Pleaded guilty. Sentenced May 2, 2017, by Judge Jeffrey P. Minehart. Defense attorney Trevan Borum requested probation only; ADA Kelly Harrell requested 5–10 years state prison. Minehart gave a county prison sentence (rather than state) citing first offense and 33 letters of support: 11 months 15 days to 23 months. Post-release: 10 years reporting probation; no contact with juveniles; lifetime sex offender registration (in-person report 4x/year).

NOTE: Heroin addiction explicitly invoked at sentencing; defendant and defense both attributed the offense in part to active addiction. Victim subsequently committed a felony and was in juvenile custody at time of sentencing. DNA evidence from furniture and vehicle seating is forensically notable. Amato warned victim against disclosure during active school investigation — concealment and potential witness-tampering.
