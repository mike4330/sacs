Former math teacher at Northeast High School, Clarksville, Tennessee. Employed by Clarksville-Montgomery County School System (CMCSS) since 2013. One victim, age 17 at time of offense (gender not specified in sources). Between November 2019 and June 23, 2020, Alvarez used her position as a teacher to engage the student in sexual contact. She also engaged in online webcam communications with the teen and simulated sexual activity. Age approximately 44 at time of offense.

Resigned from CMCSS May 26, 2021. Indicted by the September 2021 term of the Montgomery County Grand Jury. Arrested September 16, 2021 on charges of sexual battery by an authority figure (forcible fondling) and sexual exploitation of a minor. Alvarez entered a plea and was granted judicial diversion under T.C.A. §40-35-313, which allows completion of probation in lieu of a conviction being entered on the record.

On August 7, 2023, Alvarez filed a Petition for Declaratory Order with the Tennessee State Board of Education (In the Matter of: Liliana Alvarez, No. 07.02-233872), contesting the Board's effort to revoke her teaching license. The Board's Rule 0520-02-03-.09(1)(a) defines 'conviction' to include judicial diversion; Alvarez argued the rule conflicts with the diversion statute and cited Rodriguez v. State, 437 S.W.3d 450. Outcome of the license proceeding not confirmed in available sources.

NOTE: Judicial diversion is a no-conviction outcome conditional on successful probation completion — maps to no-prison in DB conventions. The license revocation dispute is analytically significant: illustrates how diversion statutes can create gaps between criminal and regulatory accountability for licensed professionals. The online/webcam conduct component adds a digital-only contact element alongside the in-person relationship.
