Judge William L. Witham Jr.

Educational diagnostician in the Special Services department at Smyrna Middle School, Smyrna School District, Kent County, Delaware. Married with two children; husband was about to be deployed overseas. Three master's degrees, 14 years of educational experience, annual salary approximately $70,000. Age 37 at time of offense. No prior criminal record.

In May 2017, a 17-year-old male student who had been reported missing was found in Brooks's car in Ocean City. The boy had previously been a student at Smyrna Middle School. Investigation revealed a sexual relationship between them that had developed from his time at the school. Further investigation uncovered a second victim, also a former student. Brooks lent her car to the first victim (who was a runaway from home), provided him with up to $150 per week in cash, and bought him an iPhone with cell phone service. Prosecutors described her as selecting students to 'wine and dine to get them into her web,' choosing victims she knew were unlikely to cooperate with investigators.

Arrested June 1, 2017; resigned from the school district the same month. Pleaded guilty November 2017 to two counts of rape in the fourth degree. Sentenced February 14, 2018 by Superior Court Resident Judge William L. Witham Jr. to four years probation with Level III intensive supervision. The DOJ had sought four years in prison. Ordered to register as a Tier II (moderate risk) sex offender, surrender her teaching certification and license, pay $354 in fines, and have no contact with minors other than her biological children.

Defense presented mental health testimony: childhood sexual abuse beginning at age 11, bipolar disorder, and a diagnosis of 'Sitting Duck Syndrome' — described as making her 'uniquely vulnerable to re-enact' prior trauma. A mental health evaluation found no measurable risk of re-offense. Defense noted she confessed before grand jury indictment, sparing victims from testifying. The prosecution countered that 'the defendant only confessed after she was caught.'

NOTE: Brooks's role in Special Services gave her structured access to students with educational and developmental challenges, though the articles do not explicitly identify the victims as having special needs. Prosecution described her as using her professional training to select and manipulate students least likely to cooperate with investigators — a deliberate targeting strategy. Victim 1 was 17 at discovery but had known Brooks since middle school; start-of-offense age unclear. Second victim age not reported. Victim age omitted from DB rather than guessed.
