New Jersey v. Leahey #259 sentenced

State New Jersey
Court Morris County Superior Court
Judge Salem V. Ahto
Incident Date 2013-01-01
Defendant Age 31
Victim Age 16
Prison Sentence 5.0 yrs

Former English teacher at Parsippany Hills High School, Parsippany, New Jersey. Age approximately 31 at time of offense; 35 at sentencing.

Victim: 16-year-old male student (a junior). Relationship began in late January 2013 with sexually explicit text messages; Leahey also sent explicit photographs of herself to the student. Acts occurred on and off school grounds through June 6, 2013.

Charged in 2013; indicted in 2014 on two counts second-degree endangering the welfare of a child, one count second-degree sexual assault, one count second-degree official misconduct, and three counts fourth-degree criminal sexual conduct. Trial was set for January 17, 2017. Leahey had previously rejected a plea offer of 5 years with full 5-year parole bar on a guilty plea to official misconduct. As jury selection was about to begin, she pled guilty to one count of endangering the welfare of a child by engaging in conduct that would debauch or impair the morals of a minor. Remaining charges dismissed as part of the plea.

Sentenced April 10, 2017 by Judge Salem V. Ahto, Morris County Superior Court, to five years in state prison. Parole eligible after approximately 13 months under New Jersey law. Morris County Supervising Assistant Prosecutors Meg Rodriguez and Sean Gaynor; County Prosecutor Fredric M. Knapp; defense attorney Judson Hand.

At sentencing, Leahey claimed the student had sexually assaulted her and that she had sent explicit texts 'because I thought it would end the ongoing sexual assault.' Rodriguez responded that the text messages showed Leahey 'flirting back' and that the victim-blaming 'came later, when the crocodile tears didn't work.' Ahto: 'She was the teacher... she is the adult.' Victim (then 20) was present at sentencing; his statement read aloud: 'No 16-year-old should go through what I went through.' Victim also filed a civil lawsuit against Leahey and the school district.

NOTE: Leahey rejected the original 5-year/no-parole plea deal, went to trial preparation, then pled at the last moment to a lesser charge with far earlier parole eligibility (~13 months vs. 5 years). Defense attempted to introduce character witnesses who disparaged the victim; prosecutor sought to invoke rape shield law to limit such evidence. Judge had spent hours analyzing emoticons and abbreviated text symbols before trial. Defendant age at offense inferred from sentencing age (35 in April 2017 → ~31 in early 2013).
