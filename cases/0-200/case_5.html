Stephanie Peterson #5 sentenced

StateFloridaCourtVolusia County Circuit Court
JudgeRaul ZambranoIncident Date2017-11-01
Defendant Age27Victim Age14
Prison Sentence3.0 yrs

Former teacher at New Smyrna Beach Middle School, New Smyrna Beach, Florida (Volusia County Schools). Age 27 at sentencing; approximately 26–27 at time of offense.

Victim: 14-year-old male student. Relationship began November 1, 2017, after Peterson lent the student her smartphone to check his Instagram account; he left himself signed in and she messaged him on his account. Communication migrated to Snapchat. Peterson sent nude photographs including a shower selfie in which her face was clearly visible. The student would sneak out at night; they had sexual intercourse in her car in his driveway, on her couch, and twice in a barn behind his home. Conduct ended January 31, 2018.

Discovered when a different teacher called the student's home to offer tutoring; his mother jokingly asked whether the teacher was going to molest him, and the student burst into tears and said "Anyone could molest you." Peterson subsequently told the student to "Delete everything" on Snapchat.

Arrested February 28, 2018. Peterson attempted suicide in the back of the police car and later contemplated using a gun.

Pleaded guilty October 16, 2018 to one count lewd and lascivious battery (sex act with a child, 2nd-degree felony) and one count electronic transmission of material harmful to minors (3rd-degree felony); a second lewd or lascivious sex battery count was dismissed. Plea range 5–10 years. Defense attorneys Aaron Delgado and William Hathaway requested no prison; prosecutor sought 6–10 years.

Sentenced by Judge Raul Zambrano to 3 years prison (below the 5-year plea minimum), 2 years community control (house arrest), 5 years sex offender probation, lifetime sex offender registration. Zambrano cited bipolar disorder as mitigation: "You are mentally ill and you're going to be mentally ill for the rest of your life." Psychologist Lisa Potash testified Peterson was in a manic state during the offense, that her motivation was attention-seeking and delusional rather than sexual gratification, and assessed low risk of reoffense.

Defense originally argued the student was a "willing participant" but withdrew this at sentencing. Victim's mother testified he had become reserved and angry and was being taunted by classmates.

NOTE: sentence_years = 3. Below-minimum sentence attributed to diagnosed bipolar disorder and manic-episode framing — defense's mental health argument succeeded where it rarely does. Incident date = offense period start (November 1, 2017).
