Former art teacher and Vocational Technology Department chair at De Anza High School in Richmond, California, part of the West Contra Costa Unified School District. Resided in Martinez. Age 40 at the time of arrest in May 2021; 42 at sentencing. The known victim was a female student who had a documented special-educational-needs designation; she met Shetterly during her freshman year of high school, and the abuse occurred when Shetterly was conducting private tutoring sessions at her own Martinez home throughout 2021.

According to police affidavits and court records, Shetterly groomed the victim through a sustained pattern of physical and emotional intimacy. The victim told investigators that she and Shetterly had kissed more than one hundred times. Shetterly wrote her love letters, bought her alcohol, and took her to sex shops. The grooming culminated in a coercive coordination scheme that is unique within the corpus: Shetterly told the victim to falsely accuse her own parents of abuse so that Shetterly herself could adopt her, and went so far as to script messages for the victim to submit to authorities asserting the false allegations.

The victim's mother discovered evidence of the abuse and reported it to the Martinez Police Department, which received the tip on May 29, 2021. Shetterly was arrested on May 30, 2021 in Martinez and booked into the Martinez Jail; initial bail was set at $250,000. The West Contra Costa Unified School District terminated her employment after the arrest. On June 30, 2022, the Contra Costa County District Attorney's Office filed twelve felony counts against her, breaking down as three counts of lewd act upon a child, one count of contacting a minor for lewd purposes, and eight counts of specific sexual acts on a minor. Shetterly turned herself in approximately two weeks after the charges were filed; she was held on $1.1 million bail. She was arraigned before Contra Costa County Superior Court Judge Rebecca Hardie on July 11, 2022 and pleaded not guilty.

On November 23, 2022, in a negotiated agreement, Shetterly entered a no-contest plea to two counts of sexual penetration with a foreign object under the aggravated provision applicable when the defendant is more than ten years older than the victim. The remaining ten counts were dismissed. The court sentenced her the same day to three years and eight months in state prison, with the sentence reducible by up to fifty percent for good behavior; she received credit for time served in the county jail prior to transfer to the California Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation. The court also imposed a ten-year stay-away order with respect to the victim and a ten-year minimum sex-offender registration requirement.

The victim's family pursued a parallel civil action in federal court against the West Contra Costa Unified School District, alleging that district officials had missed or ignored warning signs of the abuse and had failed to stop Shetterly from contacting the victim after the conduct came to light. Civil counsel Micha Star Liberty publicly characterized the criminal charges as a first step toward broader institutional accountability.

NOTE: The adoption-scheme element — coaching the victim to falsely accuse her parents of abuse so that the defendant could gain custody, with scripted messages prepared for submission to authorities — is unique within this corpus and is the strongest documented instance of attempted institutional capture by an offender. Special-needs victim adds a population-vulnerability dimension to the institutional-trust scope rationale. Substantial charge reduction (twelve felonies to two convictions) is consistent with corpus-wide patterns where penetrative conduct is documented in plea-stage records but resolved through reduced count exposure. The civil suit naming the school district is one of a small number of corpus cases with documented institutional negligence claims.
