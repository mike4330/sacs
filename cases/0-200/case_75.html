Amber Turnage #75 sentenced

StateMississippiCourtHinds County Circuit Court
JudgeHon. Adrienne Hooper-WootenIncident Date2017-01-01
Defendant Age42Victim Age17
Prison Sentence10.0 yrs

Former biology/science teacher at Provine High School, Jackson Public Schools, Jackson, Mississippi. Fifteen years of teaching experience; also served as academic liaison for student athletes. Married at time of offenses. Fired by JPS. Victim: K.W., 17-year-old male student. Turnage also tutored K.W.'s sister in biology, providing a secondary access route.

Offense period: November 1, 2016 – April 27, 2017. K.W. testified that sexual contact occurred over 100 times. Grooming included gift cards (Belk reward card, School Aid gift card, SteinMart card) and cash. Encounters took place at different motels in Jackson. Physical evidence: two videos entered at trial — oral sex in a truck and kissing at Walmart.

Discovery: Turnage's husband confronted K.W. at an AT&T store, pulled a gun, and threatened to kill him. K.W.'s father then became suspicious, searched his son's phone, and found the videos. Turnage was never questioned by police. She was indicted May 18, 2017. The gap from investigation to trial was approximately four years.

At trial, Turnage claimed K.W. brought a gun and forced her, then blackmailed her with the video. The jury rejected this defense on 2 of 6 counts. Found guilty March 11, 2021 by a Hinds County jury; taken into custody immediately. A JNOV motion was filed April 23, 2021 and denied September 27, 2021. Sentenced April 8, 2021: 15 years MDOC, 10 to serve with 5 suspended; SORA registration. Both counts concurrent. Appeal filed October 27, 2021 (Lindsey brief). Affirmed by Mississippi Court of Appeals, No. 2021-KA-01229-COA, November 29, 2022. Prosecutor: ADA Gwen Agho (Hinds County DA Jody Edward Owens II).

NOTE: One of the few jury trial verdicts (not a plea) in this DB. Turnage's coercion/blackmail defense is the most elaborate victim-blaming claim in the DB; the jury rejected it. The over-100-incidents figure and four-year gap to trial reflect a complex, contested case. The husband's armed confrontation as the indirect discovery mechanism is unique in this DB. Cash-and-gift-card grooming adds to the transactional grooming cluster (cf. Bardfield #46, Rockensies #47).
