Former special-education teacher at John Burroughs Middle School in the Valley Village area of Los Angeles, employed by the Los Angeles Unified School District. A 25-year veteran of LAUSD. Married. Age 42-43 at the time of the offense conduct (44 at sentencing). The known victim was a male student of hers who was 12 when she first met him and 13 when the abuse began.

According to the Los Angeles County District Attorney's Office, Matarico groomed the victim with special classroom attention and provided him with marijuana, then convinced him to have sexual intercourse in her vehicle. She told him he was "the only one she would go to jail for," referred to him as her "crystal meth," and said she planned to leave her husband for him. After learning of the criminal investigation, she escalated rather than withdrew — driving to the victim's new school in an attempt to make contact with him there and sending Instagram messages to the victim and his friends expressing romantic feelings. Most of the documented sexual conduct preceded her November 2024 arrest; one source places the bulk of the abuse in late 2025, but that timing is inconsistent with the arrest date and is most plausibly a journalistic error.

Matarico was arrested on November 7, 2024 by the Los Angeles Police Department and arraigned November 12, 2024 before Judge Susan J. De Witt of the Los Angeles County Superior Court. Eight felony counts were filed: five counts of lewd act upon a child under 14, two counts of lewd act upon a child age 14 or 15, and one count of furnishing cannabis to a minor. Maximum statutory exposure was approximately 20 years and 4 months. Bail was set at $590,000 and she was released on GPS monitoring; the case was prosecuted by Los Angeles County Deputy District Attorney Ariel Anson, with Deputy Public Defender Carlos Bido initially appointed before private counsel Leonard Levine took over the defense.

Matarico entered a no-contest plea before sentencing to two counts of lewd act upon a child under 14; the remaining six felony counts (three under-14 lewd-act counts, both 14-or-15 lewd-act counts, and the cannabis-to-a-minor count) were dismissed pursuant to the plea agreement. On April 27, 2026 the court sentenced her to ten years in state prison, with 616 days of pretrial credit for time served at the Los Angeles County Jail, and entered a no-contact order with the victim that explicitly extended to electronic and third-party communication. When the ten-year sentence was announced she smiled, laughed, and shook her head; her only spoken words at the hearing were "Good morning" and "Yes." Defense counsel Leonard Levine afterward described her as remorseful and said he was confident she would not contact the victim.

NOTE: Charge structure changed substantially at plea — eight original felony counts to two convictions, with the cannabis-provision count dismissed despite being structurally distinct from the sex offenses. The "crystal meth" pet name for the victim and the post-investigation escalation pattern (driving to the victim's new school, Instagram outreach to the victim and his friends) are striking grooming and concealment-failure features. The defendant's in-court reaction at sentencing — smiling and laughing as a ten-year prison term was announced — is noted in the source reporting and is the basis for the no-remorse tag, even as defense counsel described her as remorseful in post-hearing comments. The article provides a four-month abuse window in late 2025 attributed to the criminal complaint, but that span is inconsistent with the November 2024 arrest and may be a journalistic error.
