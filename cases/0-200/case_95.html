Teacher at Middle Township Elementary School in Middle Township, New Jersey (Cape May County). Age 27 at the start of the documented abuse in January 2018; 34 at the time of her January 2025 arrest. The known victim was a male student in the school whose family had befriended Caron; the institutional connection is layered, because Caron occupied two overlapping positions of trust over the same child — classroom teacher at the school and informal household caregiver to the family. The court described her formal status with respect to the children as that of a "resource family parent," New Jersey's designation for a relative or family-friend caregiver who takes children into her home outside the formal foster-care licensing path.

The arrangement progressed in stages. The victim and his siblings began staying overnight at Caron's home one to two nights a week; in 2016 the children moved in with her on a permanent basis and continued to live in her home through 2020. The sexual abuse of the victim is documented to have begun in approximately January 2018, when the victim was 11 and Caron was 27. The victim was 13 and Caron was 28 when she became pregnant by him; their child was born in 2019 and was approximately five years old at the time the criminal investigation opened in late 2024.

The case surfaced through an unusual discovery vector. In December 2024, the victim's biological father posted on social media observing that Caron's five-year-old child closely resembled his son. The post prompted a criminal investigation. The victim, by then an older teenager, confirmed the sexual relationship in a police interview on January 3, 2025. Caron was arrested on January 15, 2025 and charged with aggravated sexual assault, sexual assault of a victim under 13, and endangering the welfare of a child. Middle Township School District placed her on paid administrative leave the same day. She made a virtual first appearance the next morning, January 16, before a court that identified her on the record as the resource-family parent for the victim's family.

A Cape May County grand jury returned a true bill on June 17, 2025 on three counts: aggravated sexual assault by a person with supervisory or disciplinary power over the victim (first-degree), sexual assault of a victim under 13 with a defendant four or more years older (second-degree), and endangering the welfare of a child by sexual conduct (second-degree). The case has been actively litigated in pretrial posture since: a pretrial conference was held before Cape May County Superior Court Judge Jeffrey J. Waldman in March 2026, with a case review scheduled for August 24, 2026 and trial set to begin September 8, 2026. Defense counsel is John W. Tumelty; the case is being prosecuted by Cape May County Assistant Prosecutor Dara Paley.

NOTE: Major case in the corpus on multiple dimensions. The institutional position of trust is doubled — formal teacher at the school and resource-family caregiver in the home — which complicates conventional analyses that assume a single access vector; the falsification test holds either way (but-for the teacher-and-then-caregiver relationship, the victim would not have been in Caron's home for four years), and the layering itself is analytically distinctive. The child born of the relationship constitutes contemporaneous biological evidence of penetrative conduct that does not depend on victim testimony, which is the basis for coding contact_type=3 on the relevant counts. The discovery vector — paternity-by-physical-resemblance flagged on social media by the biological father six years after the child's birth — is unique within the corpus and may itself be worth tagging if a comparable second case appears. Victim cohabitation in the defendant's home for approximately four years places this case at the extreme end of the institutional-residence pattern and supports the `victim-in-home` and `adoption-scheme` tags simultaneously.
