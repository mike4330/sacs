Former special education teacher and volunteer soccer coach at Downers Grove South High School in Downers Grove, Illinois, in Community High School District 99. Born 1994. She was 28 when the alleged abuse began in January 2023 and 30 when she was arrested in March 2025. She was married at the time. The boy was her soccer student and her tutoring student.

Prosecutors allege that she began contacting him when he was 14 and that the abuse ran from January 2023 through August or September 2024. They allege about fifty sexual acts, at least forty-five of them at the school, including during school hours, and others at her home, along with grooming text messages. The initial complaint was narrower. Court records described sexual intercourse in a classroom in December 2023, when the boy was 15 and she was 28. In that early account he said he last spoke with her in February 2024 and that they both acknowledged it was wrong. On April 17, 2025, he and his parents returned to the DuPage County Children's Advocacy Center, and he disclosed a longer history. The later charges cover that longer span.

His mother found the messages after she replaced a broken phone. When she signed into his iCloud account, a thread appeared under a number with no name. He and his parents reported it to the Downers Grove Police Department on March 15, 2025. Police arrested Formella during a traffic stop the next day and first charged her with two counts of aggravated criminal sexual abuse and one count of criminal sexual assault. At her March 17 first appearance the court denied the state's request to detain her. She was released on conditions barring contact with the victim or anyone under 18 and barring her from the school. The district placed her on paid administrative leave, and the principal notified families.

Detectives seized her phone. Court records say they found diary entries in the Notes app about the student. In the interview she adamantly denied having sex with him. She said everybody comes after her because she is good looking, and she is just a good person, and that she cared about him. She also said she knew the relationship was wrong but could not stop it because she cared about him so much. She told detectives the boy had blackmailed her. She said he took her unattended phone, entered a passcode he had seen her use, sent the sexual messages to his own phone, and deleted them from hers, and that she had not known the messages existed. She said he was stalking her and that her husband knew. Her husband told detectives he knew nothing about it. She said the Notes entries were how she processed anxiety and that any references to sex were not about the boy.

On April 14, 2025, she pleaded not guilty to the original three counts before Judge Mia McPherson. Her attorney, Todd Pugh, declined to comment. The case was continued to June 17. Prosecutors said the assault count would carry four to 15 years and would not be probationable, that the abuse counts would be probationable, and that a conviction on any of the three would require sex-offender registration.

A DuPage County grand jury returned a 52-count indictment on May 20, 2025: twenty counts of criminal sexual assault, twenty counts of aggravated criminal sexual abuse, six counts of indecent solicitation of a child, and six counts of grooming. The indictment stayed sealed until June 17. On June 16 she resigned from the district and voluntarily surrendered her teaching license. On June 17 she was arraigned on the new counts, pleaded not guilty, and faced 55 counts in all. Assistant state's attorney Jaclyn McAndrew asked again that she be detained and told the court that investigators had found hundreds, if not thousands, of text messages. Defense attorney Richard Kayne said she had complied with the March conditions and that the indictment had been sealed since May 20. Judge McPherson called the case horrifying, shocking, and appalling. She found that the community at large was not threatened, denied detention, and added conditions. Those conditions require monitoring software on Formella's phones, tablets, computers, and email, a GPS monitor to keep her away from the boy's home, school, and workplace, no travel outside Illinois without permission, surrender of her passport, no entry to Downers Grove South, and no contact with minors. Prosecutors said a conviction on all counts would expose her to as much as 60 years. She has not been sentenced.

In early July 2025 the court denied Pugh's request to cut a 5,000-foot GPS exclusion around the boy's home, school, and workplace down to 2,500 feet. The exclusion covered the home she shared with her husband, Michael Formella, and she had been staying with her parents.

She remains on pretrial release. A status hearing was set for September 23, 2026, before Judge McPherson. That date has passed. As of mid-October 2026, no trial date had been reported.
