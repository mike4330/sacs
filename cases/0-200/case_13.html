English teacher at Daniel Hand High School, Madison, Connecticut (New Haven County). Canadian citizen and mother of two. Age 39 at sentencing.

Two male victims. The younger victim (age 14) was contacted via Instagram with flirtatious and sexual messages beginning in early 2014. Marchese told him she could not sleep thinking about him and called him out of lunch and free periods. Messaging continued into summer 2014. When he asked her to stop, she messaged nonstop, then backed off and resurfaced two days before the new school year saying he broke her heart. She later apologized, saying she was drunk when she sent the message.

The older victim (age 17) received a provocative selfie through Instagram showing Marchese in thong underwear. In December 2014, the two kissed and Marchese performed oral sex on him in a locked classroom. The student subsequently attempted to blackmail Marchese for $200. Marchese told her father, whom she described as abusive with Mafia ties, and allegedly threatened the student would never play football again.

When interviewed by police, Marchese admitted the selfie was of her but claimed she sent it to her husband and did not know how a student obtained it.

Originally charged with second-degree sexual assault, risk of injury to a minor, and two counts of sexual misconduct with a student. Entered Alford plea (does not admit guilt but acknowledges sufficient evidence for conviction) to lesser charges: two counts first-degree unlawful restraint, two counts second-degree reckless endangerment, one count second-degree harassment, one count second-degree threatening.

Sentenced to 3 years prison, 5 years probation, 10 years sex offender registry, sex offender treatment. Faces possible deportation to Canada under federal immigration law. Judge Melanie Cradle presiding. Prosecutor David Strollo. Defense attorney William F. Dow III.

Ex-husband Robert Marchese, a teacher at the same school, was arrested January 2015 for disorderly conduct and interfering with an emergency call; entered family violence education program.

Both victims filed civil lawsuits against the Town of Madison and Madison Board of Education for negligent supervision, seeking damages in excess of $15,000.

NOTE: Significant charge reduction — sexual assault and risk of injury to minor dismissed in exchange for non-sex felony pleas. Alford plea. Oral sex on campus (locked classroom). Grooming via Instagram. Defendant alcohol noted (drunk message). Blackmail counter-claim by older victim.
