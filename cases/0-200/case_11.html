Former academic support counselor at St. Xavier High School (all-boys Jesuit school), Cincinnati, Ohio (Hamilton County). Age 42 at time of offense; 43 at sentencing. Married with three children; marriage ended following arrest.

Victim: 17-year-old male student assigned to Nutley's academic support program in fall 2023. Nutley initiated contact outside school hours beginning November 2023. Conduct included sexually explicit texts, nude photographs, sexual encounters at least four times both on and off campus, a $100/month cash allowance, food, and test answers sent in advance. In a November 30, 2023 text she said it would be "wild" to perform a sex act on the student "when the whole school is at Mass." In March 2024 the student tried to halt the relationship; Nutley continued contacting him via text.

By October 2024 rumors had reached staff, who reported to the school principal. The student disclosed to his parents that month and met with school administrators. St. Xavier announced on October 11, 2024 that Nutley had been fired following an internal investigation. Indicted shortly after on six counts of sexual battery (3rd-degree felony), facing up to 15 years.

Pleaded guilty June 10, 2025 to two counts of sexual battery; sentenced by Judge Jennifer Branch, Hamilton County Common Pleas Court, to 3 years prison, 5 years probation, Tier III sex offender registration every 90 days for life. Assistant Prosecutor Elyse Deters read victim impact statement: "I have a hard time trusting people, especially authority figures."

Nutley was tearful and submitted a letter to the court stating she was "sick with shame," but Branch told her: "I was looking for you to say that you acknowledge the harm that you caused this young man... You pretty much ruined the rest of his high school career. I didn't see it. I didn't hear it." Prosecutors' sentencing memorandum stated Nutley had failed to show remorse, had "completely blamed the victim" in a psychological interview, and that evidence pointed to grooming of other children. Defense characterized her as a "broken woman" suffering from mental illness and alcoholism.

Catholic private school institutional response: prompt disclosure by staff once rumors surfaced, immediate firing, public statement urging other victims to come forward.
