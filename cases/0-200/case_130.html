School social worker and IEP case manager for Jefferson County Public Schools, Lakewood, CO. Rotated across six JeffCo schools 2021–2024; worked at Three Creeks K-8 (January 2022–May 2024), where she served as the victim's IEP case manager. Also worked at Columbine Hills Elementary, Leawood Elementary, Manning Middle, Drake Middle, and Wheat Ridge High. Age 26–27 at start of relationship; 28 at arrest. Resident of Lakewood, CO.

Castro met the victim at the end of his sixth grade year (approximately late 2022, when he was 12). She served as his IEP case manager, giving her regular one-on-one access. She asked him to be her "boyfriend" around age 12–13. Physical contact — cuddling, kissing, intimate touching, and exchange of nude photographs — occurred in her school office. Castro told the victim they "couldn't have sex until he turned 18" and discussed moving together to New Mexico or California when he turned 18 "to start a new life." Castro saved herself in the victim's contacts as "youth pastor" to avoid parental detection. Communication was conducted via Instagram over approximately two years.

Parents discovered "countless" inappropriate messages on the victim's phone on October 29, 2024. Arvada Police Department arrested Castro November 6, 2024. Bond set at 5,000; remanded into custody at plea.

Originally charged with Sexual Assault on a Child by One in a Position of Trust — Victim Less Than 15 with Pattern of Abuse (F3) and Internet Luring of a Child (F4). Pleaded guilty February 2, 2026 to Sexual Assault on a Child by One in a Position of Trust — Victim Less Than 15 (added Count 3, F3). Original counts dismissed.

Sentenced April 2, 2026: 20 years to life Sex Offender Intensive Supervision Probation (ISP), with 3 years in community corrections at Intervention Community Corrections Services (ICCS) as a condition of probation. Lifetime sex offender registration. 4,430 restitution. Remanded to ICCS. The court acknowledged dozens of letters from community members requesting the maximum sentence. Prosecutor: "There is no sentence that I could ask for which will give the community their trust back in Jefferson County Public Schools, and there is not a sentence that will take away the trauma this child suffered." Castro told the court she is committed to treatment and "will never work with children again."
