State v. Anna Crocker #21 sentenced

Former teacher aide in the Twin Lakes area of Kenosha County, Wisconsin. Mother of four. Age 34 at the time of her February 2026 sentencing; offenses against the four known male victims spanned the winter of 2023-2024 through October 2024. Defendant_age and victim_age in this record reflect the principal sleepover-assault incident; the case as a whole involved four boys ranging in age from approximately 12 to 14.

Victim 1 was a 12-year-old current student. During the winter of 2023-2024 he attended a sleepover at Crocker's home and fell asleep in the basement; according to prosecutors, Crocker woke him, removed his pants, and sexually assaulted him while other children present at the sleepover witnessed what happened. The victim told investigators he repeatedly told Crocker to get off and tried to push her away. Months later, Crocker sent him a text message instructing him to apologize to her 'for what happened in the basement' — prosecutors described this as her trying to make him feel guilty — and told him he could not tell anyone.

Victim 2 was a 14-year-old former student. He reported that Crocker had been inappropriate with him and that the two had exchanged sexual chats and photos through Snapchat. He further reported that in August 2024 Crocker sexually assaulted him in a parking lot. In a subsequent interview with detectives, Crocker admitted receiving a sexual video from the 14-year-old, saving it, and forwarding it to another student at his school. Detectives seized her phone and recovered the video from a password-protected folder.

Victim 3 was a 13-year-old who told a member of his school's staff that Crocker had sent him inappropriate messages, including multiple videos of herself performing a sex act. Twin Lakes police interviewed him and his parents on October 10, 2024.

A fourth victim was acknowledged in the charging language and at sentencing but specific details were not reported publicly.

The Kenosha County Sheriff's Department and Twin Lakes Police Department investigated; the alleged conduct involved both a current student and former students of the school where Crocker worked. Crocker was arrested in October 2024.

On February 17, 2026, Crocker pleaded guilty to multiple sex crimes against the four boys. At sentencing the same day, Kenosha County Circuit Judge David Hughes imposed 51.5 years of incarceration plus 32 years of extended supervision, with lifetime sex-offender registration. Hughes characterized the conduct in plain terms from the bench: 'She sought out children, she used children and she took advantage of children.' Prosecutor Rosa Delgado, of the Kenosha County District Attorney's Office, told the court Crocker 'couldn't help herself' and was 'juggling all these boys at the same time.'

In allocution Crocker expressed remorse, telling the court through tears: 'I take full responsibility for what has taken place. I am disturbed by the harm and distress, along with the trauma that you have to endure. I am deeply sorry.' She also pleaded for mercy, asking for a lighter sentence even as she acknowledged the seriousness of the offenses. The judge declined to moderate. One victim's mother addressed the court — through a court order shielding the victims and families from being recorded — saying, 'This person destroyed my son's psyche and loving and caring spirit.' That family subsequently moved out of state. Crocker showed little visible emotion as the sentence was read; she will be in her 80s by the time the incarceration portion runs.

NOTE: Major sentence in the corpus — 51.5 years incarceration is among the longest recorded. Multiple distinguishing features: four-victim scope; mixed online and physical conduct (Snapchat sexting, CSAM-like distribution of a victim's self-recorded video to another student, in-person sexual assault, and a sleepover attack with child witnesses); evidence-tampering element in the post-assault apology-extraction text to Victim 1; multiple students of the school involved as both victims and incidental witnesses.
