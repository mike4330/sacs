State v. Alexandria Vera #9 sentenced

StateTexasCourtHarris County District Court
JudgeMichael McSpadden Incident Date2015-06-01
Defendant Age22 Victim Age13
Prison Sentence10.0 yrs

Former English teacher at Stovall Middle School (8th grade), Aldine Independent School District, north Houston, Harris County, Texas. Victim was a 13-year-old male, 8th grader. Age 22 at time of offense. Vera has a young daughter who knew about the relationship and called the 13-year-old 'dad.'

Vera met the student in summer school in 2015, with initial contact via Instagram. She told police she initially resisted the boy's advances, then agreed to 'hang out' with him in fall 2015. The sexual relationship that followed involved sex almost every day for nine months. Vera told investigators she and the victim 'love each other.' During a fall open house, she was introduced to the victim's parents as 'his girlfriend.' She subsequently moved the teen and his father into her home and told neighbors she was dating the father. She bought groceries for the family and paid their phone bills. She told neighbors the boy was her brother.

In January 2016, Vera became pregnant by the student. The boy's family was described as 'very supportive and excited.' In early 2016, the school principal received a tip about Vera. CPS questioned her in February 2016, after which she obtained an abortion. She was arrested in June 2016 and originally charged with continuous sexual abuse of a child, which carried a potential life sentence. Prosecutors described her conduct as a pattern of deception: 'She has a history of straight-out deception' (prosecutor Denise Nichols).

On November 16, 2016, Vera pleaded guilty to the lesser charge of aggravated sexual assault of a child (Texas Penal Code §22.021) under an agreement capping the maximum at 30 years. Defense sought probation. A mental health professional, Karen Lawson, testified on Vera's behalf that she grew up around domestic violence and was driven by a need to be loved.

Sentenced January 13, 2017 by 209th District Court Judge Michael McSpadden to 10 years in prison. McSpadden stated he did not believe Vera was a danger to children or a 'classic pedophile,' but wanted to send a message to the community: 'We want our educators to teach our students. We want them to keep their hands off students.' Vera is eligible for parole after five years.

The victim, by then 14, was placed in foster care through at least August 2017. His parents — who accepted the relationship and celebrated the pregnancy — were ordered to undergo therapy and parent education classes but were not charged.
