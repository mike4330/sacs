Former teacher at the Warren Area Elementary Center and assistant volleyball coach at Warren Area High School in Warren County, Pennsylvania (Warren County School District). Resided in Erie at the time of charging. Age 25 at the time of the documented offense in 2021; 27 at sentencing in August 2023. The known victim was a female high-school student; her exact age is not specified in available reporting; victim age recorded as 16 as a high-school-context inferred estimate.

Investigators learned of the conduct through a third-party report of suspected abuse that, in the affidavit of probable cause, "stated concerns over possible grooming behaviors." The victim was subsequently interviewed at the Warren County Children's Advocacy Center forensic-interview facility. Investigators determined that Lasecki had made alcohol and marijuana available to the victim, exposed her to sexually explicit material via the internet on more than one occasion, and touched her inappropriately while in Lasecki's car at a remote location in Warren County. Assistant District Attorney Casey Strickland told the court that Lasecki had been able to "ingratiate" herself into the private life of the victim through the victim's vulnerabilities: "Lasecki had a choice to do what she did. The victim did not."

Lasecki was arrested in approximately late June 2022 and arraigned on July 1, 2022 before District Judge Laura Bauer; she was released on $25,000 unsecured bail. The Pennsylvania Sexual Offenders Assessment Board evaluated her ahead of sentencing and concluded she did not meet the threshold for the sexually-violent-predator designation, but characterized the underlying conduct as predatory in nature.

On August 25, 2023 in the Warren County Court of Common Pleas, Lasecki was sentenced by Judge Gregory Hammond to two years probation on the indecent-assault count and an additional two years probation on the corruption-of-minors count, for a combined four years of probation, plus sex-offender registration, DNA collection, a no-contact order, sexual-offender counseling, mental-health and drug/alcohol evaluations, seventy-five hours of community service, and a bar on any employment or volunteering with minors. No incarceration was imposed. Defense attorney Elizabeth Feronti noted publicly that the prosecution had previously indicated probation was "certainly appropriate." Thirty character letters were submitted on Lasecki's behalf at sentencing; she declined to address the court.

Judge Hammond addressed the victim directly from the bench: "You're 100 percent the victim. The fact you believe the community turned against you is heartbreaking" — using the moment to articulate why victims often do not come forward.

NOTE: Outcome is among the lighter dispositions in the corpus despite an SOAB finding that the conduct was predatory in nature, and despite documented multi-vector grooming (alcohol provision, marijuana provision, exposure to sexually explicit internet material, off-campus inappropriate touching). The community-backlash dimension is a feature of the public record in this case rather than a peripheral observation: the bench framed the community's reaction as a cautionary illustration of why victims withhold reports.
