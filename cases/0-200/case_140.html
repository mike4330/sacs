Former social studies teacher and assistant coach for girls volleyball and soccer at Smithson Valley High School, Comal Independent School District, Spring Branch, Texas. Age 22 at time of offense; 25 at conviction.

Rogers became romantically and sexually involved with an 18-year-old female student in late 2014 and early 2015. The victim testified to numerous sexual encounters including one on Christmas 2014. Drug use was a documented component: the victim testified they took Xanax at school and smoked marijuana and snorted cocaine on off-campus excursions. The relationship was discovered when the victim began dating another student, who discovered the affair and notified police. Comal County deputies began investigating in April 2015; Rogers resigned from Comal ISD the same month. The relationship reportedly continued even after her April 2015 arrest.

Tried by jury in Comal County District Court in November 2017. Rogers pleaded not guilty and did not testify; defense attorney John Kuntz argued there was no proof of sex, only emotional attachment. The former student testified she had loved Rogers and described the relationship. The jury deliberated approximately one hour before returning unanimous guilty verdicts on two counts of improper relationship between educator and student. The jury also recommended probation (Texas jury probation — the jury recommends community supervision, which the judge is bound to accept). Sentenced November 1, 2017 by Judge Jack Robison to 10 years community supervision on each count, concurrent; no prison; $2,500 fine per count.

NOTE: Adult victim (18 at time of offense) — the improper relationship statute makes the educator-student relationship criminal regardless of victim age. Female victim. Drug use (Xanax, marijuana, cocaine) is an unusual documented grooming and relationship factor. Same county as Massey (#137). No-prison outcome despite jury conviction on two second-degree felonies — possible because jury probation transfers the sentencing recommendation from the judge to the jury.
