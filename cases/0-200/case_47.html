Former dean of Leadership and Student Support at Corona Arts middle school in Queens, New York City. Married, mother of three, with a salary of approximately eighty-five thousand dollars a year at the time of the offense. Age 31 at the start of the documented sexual relationship in mid-2022; 33 at sentencing. The known victim was a 14-year-old male student at the school.

Rockensies first contacted the victim through the school email system, ostensibly about an unrelated issue with another student. Communications migrated to Instagram in September 2022 and from there to in-person meetings: she took the victim for a walk in a park after a supermarket trip, hugged him "really close," and kissed him. The relationship became sexual shortly afterward and continued at a pace the victim later described to investigators as approximately four times per week. Encounters typically took place in her car parked near the school, frequently during school hours.

Rockensies grooming included substantial transactional elements. The victim told investigators she gave him cash payments of up to five hundred dollars at a time and provided him with nicotine devices. She also exchanged sexually explicit text messages with him while caring for her own children at home — for example, "You're gonna be lucky once I put this kid to sleep" and "I wanna be able to kiss those lips of yours."

In November 2023 Rockensies told investigators that the victim had begun to extort her, demanding $5,000 and threatening to shoot her and her children. The victim denied the claim and said his Instagram account had been hacked after he was banned from school activity for skipping summer school. The extortion-counter-claim posture became a defining feature of the case's public narrative.

Rockensies was arrested in August 2023, with formal charges announced by Queens County District Attorney Melinda Katz in December 2023. Her husband William Rockensies publicly defended her, telling reporters: "My wife is innocent. She's the mother of three kids." On April 9, 2024 in Queens County Supreme Court, Rockensies entered a negotiated plea to a single rape count covering the statutory abuse of the 14-year-old. The court accepted the plea agreement and imposed no incarceration: the disposition required permanent surrender of her teaching license and completion of a two-year sexual-predator program. Press photographs of Rockensies smiling as she left the courthouse drew significant public criticism of the plea structure.

In January 2024 the victim's mother filed a civil lawsuit in New York State court against the New York City Department of Education, alleging that district officials had been negligent in failing to detect or stop the abuse. The plaintiff's civil counsel was Jonathan Cooper. DA Katz publicly characterized the conduct as "an abuse of authority and a betrayal of the trust students and parents place in their schools."
