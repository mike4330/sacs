Mary Faith McCormick #83 sentenced

StateArkansasCourtBenton County Circuit Court
JudgeBrad KarrenIncident Date2014
Defendant Age32Victim Age13
Prison Sentence2.0 yrs

Former 6th-grade language arts teacher at Siloam Springs Intermediate School, Siloam Springs, Arkansas (Benton County). Three years at the school; previously taught in Gravette and Little Rock. Age 32 at arrest. Victim: 13-year-old male student, described by his mother as a 4.0 student and exceptional athlete.

Two sexual encounters: one at McCormick's home and one at a remote location after she picked the victim up from a friend's house late at night. Grooming began via Snapchat approximately two weeks after the school year ended — McCormick sent a topless photo of herself; the victim sent a photo in return. A Snapchat glitch saved the images to his phone rather than deleting them; this became the primary physical evidence.

Investigation began August 8, 2014 after a tip. McCormick was arrested in August 2014 and placed on paid leave before resigning. Charged with first-degree sexual assault (Class A felony, Arkansas). Pleaded guilty July 14, 2015. Sentenced July 30, 2015 by Circuit Judge Brad Karren: 8 years ADC, parole eligible after 2 years; 12-year suspended sentence after release; SORA registration; sex offender treatment; no unsupervised contact with minors except biological children; no contact with the victim or witnesses. McCormick showed no emotion and declined to address the court. Defense attorney: Joel Huggins. Prosecutors: Carly Marshall (deputy) and Stuart Cearley (chief deputy).

Victim's mother, in an impact statement: "I have sent my child into the public school system... not ever believing that one day his very own teacher... would be the wolf that would prey upon him." The family had to close their business and relocate due to harassment and community hostility following McCormick's arrest.

NOTE: The Snapchat glitch preserving supposedly-deleted evidence is a recurring prosecution windfall in this DB; the platform's disappearing-message design failed in the same way in other cases. McCormick declining to speak at sentencing contrasts with the elaborate apologies documented in Altice #81 and Hill #82. The victim family bearing secondary harm — forced relocation and business closure — is rarely documented this explicitly in coverage.
