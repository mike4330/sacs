Former science teacher at Hephzibah Middle School, Hephzibah, Georgia. One male victim, age 13. Byrapaka sent nude photos and explicit text messages to the student discussing kissing and sexual touching, and lured him from his classroom to another room on campus where she allegedly molested him. The victim's mother discovered the correspondence. Byrapaka was brought from India to the United States at age 9; at time of arrest she was in the country on a student visa and had just completed her master's degree. Age approximately 23 at time of offense.

Arrested January 16, 2020. Initially granted $25,000 bond on child molestation and enticement charges, but ICE placed an immigration hold due to her visa status; she remained at the Charles B. Webster Detention Center without bond from the date of arrest. Indicted by Richmond County Grand Jury November 12, 2020 on five counts: sexual contact by employee or agent (1st degree), sexual contact by employee or agent (2nd degree), two counts of child molestation, and enticement of a child for indecent purposes. Held without bond for approximately 30 months.

Pleaded guilty July 14, 2022 before Judge John Flythe to two counts of criminal attempt to commit enticement of a child for indecent purposes. Prosecutor (ADA Kyle Davis) agreed to forego further incarceration given time already served and the likelihood of deportation. Sentenced to 13 years' probation; time served credited. Judge declined to apply the First Offender Act, ensuring the conviction remains on the public record. Faces deportation to India.

NOTE: Pre-trial detention of approximately 30 months served as the de facto incarceration component; formal sentence is probation-only — maps to no-prison for DB conventions, but the custody time is analytically significant. Original charges included child molestation and sexual contact by employee/agent; resolved via plea to criminal attempt/enticement, a significant charge reduction. Judge's refusal to apply First Offender Act is notable given the deportation consequence — the public conviction record was preserved over defense preference. Victim's mother's victim impact statement explicitly called out the double standard in public reactions because the victim was male.
