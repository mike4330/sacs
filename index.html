About SACS

Sexual Abuse Case Studies

What this is

SACS is a structured case database documenting criminal prosecutions of educators and other persons in positions of trust who committed sexual offenses against minors. Each case is sourced from court records, press releases, and contemporaneous news reporting.

Scope and methodology

This database intentionally tracks female-perpetrated cases exclusively. This is a deliberate analytic choice, not a sampling artifact.

Public discourse has increasingly highlighted female teacher cases as a novel or growing phenomenon. This project examines that corpus specifically — to document patterns, outcomes, and systemic failures within it.

The critical caveat: Male perpetrators are statistically dominant in educator sexual abuse. The best available research consistently places female offenders at roughly 20–25% of cases (some estimates lower). Nothing in this database should be interpreted as suggesting female perpetrators are more prevalent, more dangerous, or more noteworthy than male ones. The female-only scope is an analytic choice, not a claim about base rates. Sentencing patterns, grooming methods, and institutional responses documented here may differ from the broader population of educator abuse cases.

A note on electronic evidence

Cases drawn from the smartphone era (roughly post-2012) frequently feature incontrovertible electronic evidence: text messages, Snapchat communications, explicit photos, and video. Pre-smartphone cases were more dependent on victim testimony and were substantially harder to prosecute. This means the DB is not a consistent time series — earlier cases that appear here likely represent only the most egregious or well-documented incidents from their period, while more recent cases include a broader range of offense severity.

Data sources

Each case entry links to its source: court documents, Department of Justice press releases, state attorney general releases, and news reporting. Source quality varies; court documents and official press releases are treated as more reliable than secondary reporting.

What this is not

  • This is not a comprehensive census of female educator abuse cases. It is a curated set selected for documentation quality and analytical interest.
  • This is not a registry. The database does not republish sex offender registry data as such.
  • This is not advocacy for any particular sentencing policy. Sentencing outcomes are documented and analyzed descriptively.
